# Wales source register

Discovery and core source-check date: 8 September 2026. See LOCAL_PREVIEW_STATUS.md for completed implementation; authoring tasks below preserve the original research checklist, not a list of unimplemented website features. These sources support the content specification. Discovery is not a complete legal audit. A source's publication date, territorial application, amendments and commencement must be checked when authoring the relevant learning material.

## W01 — Care and support legislation

The [Welsh Government law summary](https://www.gov.wales/law-care-and-support-summary) and [Social Care Wales Act hub](https://socialcare.wales/resources-guidance/information-and-learning-hub/sswbact) identify the Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014 as the central framework for the Wales care/support pack.

Authoring task: inspect the current legislation and regulations linked from the hub. Map adult, child and carer assessment, eligibility, meeting needs, safeguarding and cross-border provisions separately. Do not substitute the Act's name into Care Act text.

## W02 — Codes of practice and advocacy

The [Welsh Government codes collection](https://www.gov.wales/social-services-codes-practice) provides separate sources for assessment, meeting needs, general functions, charging, partnership arrangements and other topics. [Advocacy services: code of practice](https://www.gov.wales/advocacy-services-code-practice) is a separate source for that pathway.

Authoring task: select the applicable code and regulations for each duty, record its actual version and review outstanding amendments. The collection's overall date is not a substitute for checking each linked document.

## W03 — Adult and child safeguarding

- [Working Together to Safeguard People, Volume 6: adults at risk](https://www.gov.wales/sites/default/files/publications/2019-06/volume-6-handling-individual-cases-to-protect-adults-at-risk.pdf).
- [Working Together to Safeguard People, Volume 5: children at risk](https://www.gov.wales/sites/default/files/publications/2019-05/working-together-to-safeguard-people-volume-5-handling-individual-cases-to-protect-children-at-risk.pdf).
- [Wales Safeguarding Procedures: adults at risk definitions](https://safeguarding.wales/en/adu-i/adu-i-a2/a2-p2/).
- [Wales Safeguarding Procedures: enquiries](https://www.safeguarding.wales/en/adu-i/adu-i-a3pt1/).

The adult materials identify the section 126 framework. Authoring must distinguish the statutory definition, enquiry duty and reporting duties, including section 128, rather than doing a section-number replacement. Check updated national guidance and regional procedures, and identify which agencies each duty applies to. Review child protection and child care/support routes independently.

## W04 — Mental capacity

The [Mental Capacity Act Code of Practice page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/mental-capacity-act-code-of-practice) explicitly applies to England and Wales and links an official Welsh version.

This supports sharing reviewed statutory principles. It does not establish that all local pathways, other statutes, current case law or existing app explanations can be copied unchanged.

## W05 — Liberty and restrictive practice

- [Welsh Government deprivation of liberty guidance and forms](https://www.gov.wales/mental-capacity-act-deprivation-liberty-guidance-and-forms).
- [Reducing restrictive practices framework](https://www.gov.wales/reducing-restrictive-practices-framework-html).

The forms page contains Welsh guidance and forms, including standard/urgent authorisation requests. Its displayed update date is 2015. Treat it as an identified official entry point; verify current directions, case law and local use before recommending a form. See W12 for unresolved legal-update work.

## W06 — Mental health

- [Mental Health Act 1983: Welsh Code of Practice](https://www.gov.wales/mental-health-act-1983-code-practice), identifying the revised 2016 Code.
- [Mental Health (Wales) Measure 2010](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/mwa/2010/7/pdfs/mwa_20100007_en.pdf), the original enacted text.

The Wales pack needs its own Code references and coverage of the Measure. Before drafting operational guidance, retrieve current amended provisions, regulations and guidance; the enacted PDF alone is insufficient. Check advocacy, care coordination, planning, aftercare and reform commencement separately.

## W07 — Professional standards and CPD

[Social Care Wales' current Workers' Code](https://socialcare.wales/codes-of-practice-and-guidance/code-of-professional-practice-for-social-care-workers) states that the updated Code applies from **1 July 2026**. The page was updated on 17 August 2026. Use the current Code and linked social-worker practice guidance for local standards; do not reuse SWE standard numbers.

The regulator's [1 April 2025 CPD change notice](https://socialcare.wales/news-stories/weve-changed-our-cpd-requirements-to-make-it-simpler) says that logging CPD details in SCWonline and evidencing the former 45/90-hour targets are no longer required. It retains regular manager discussions and says records may be sampled.

Product consequence: a Wales log should support reflection, impact, discussions and evidence, without a mandatory hours-target counter, SWE two-piece/peer targets or a SWE submission claim. Check the linked current FAQs and renewal guidance before finalising any required fields or renewal messages. Old PDFs stating 90 hours must not become the default rule.

## W08 — Newly qualified social workers

The [Consolidation Programme FAQ](https://socialcare.wales/qualifications-funding/social-work-qualifications/consolidation-programme-questions), updated 29 May 2025, describes the programme within the first-three-years framework. Its stated requirement includes social workers qualified in Wales on or after 1 April 2016 who continue to practise in Wales.

Authoring task: verify current requirements and the position for qualifications obtained elsewhere, exemptions and individual registration conditions. Use “Student and Newly Qualified Pathway” for the app; do not call the Welsh programme ASYE or promise that using this resource satisfies it.

## W09 — Additional learning needs and transition

The [Welsh Government ALN guidance collection](https://www.gov.wales/additional-learning-needs-guidance-schools-early-years-settings-and-local-authorities) links the ALN Code. Its [decision-making and communication guidance](https://www.gov.wales/additional-learning-needs-aln-decision-making-and-communication-html) describes ALN decisions and planning.

Authoring task: verify current ALN legislation, Code, Individual Development Plan terminology, age/setting limits, transition and challenge routes. Do not carry England SEND/EHCP workflow into Wales through acronym substitution.

## W10 — Welsh language and culturally responsive practice

The [More than just words implementation update](https://www.gov.wales/written-statement-update-more-just-words-2022-27) describes strengthening Welsh-language health and care provision so that needs are met without people having to request it.

Authoring task: incorporate language preference and the Active Offer into assessment, capacity support, participation and recording examples. Check the current plan and applicable Welsh-language duties. Distinguish service-language needs from the language in which this app is displayed.

## W11 — Rights and recording: source verification outstanding

Inspect current Equality Act and Human Rights Act provisions and applicable Welsh duties before marking shared content reviewed. Identify authoritative data-protection/information-sharing guidance and Welsh-language requirements relevant to the examples. The current England module is an inventory source, not evidence of a completed Wales review.

## W12 — Time-sensitive and cross-border topics: verification outstanding

The existing app contains assertions about a June 2026 deprivation-of-liberty judgment and phased Mental Health Act reforms. This discovery pass has not audited those assertions or established their current Welsh operational implications.

Before reusing them: inspect the judgment and subsequent authoritative guidance, check each commencement provision and territorial effect, and record the checked date. Also research cross-border placements, ordinary residence, funding responsibilities and children’s arrangements. A location selector does not determine those questions for a live case.

## Review record required for each published topic

Record the author, qualified reviewer/role, official source URLs, exact provisions/paragraphs where relevant, source publication/update dates, effective dates, territorial scope, date checked, open issues and next review date. Use separate states for source discovery, drafting, source verification, practice review and publication. Keep the original version available when a published topic is superseded.

## Completion source notes — 8 September 2026

The authored pack now distinguishes adult/child/carer assessments (sections 19/21/24), assessment versus eligibility/charging, section 126(1) definition versus 126(2) enquiries and section 128 relevant-partner reporting. Welsh Codes, safeguarding procedures and the relevant statutory guidance support these learning distinctions. The legislation website's individual amended HTML provisions were intermittently unavailable; the guide avoids claiming a comprehensive consolidated-law audit.

The current [CIW liberty judgment notice](https://www.careinspectorate.wales/supreme-court-judgment-deprivation-liberty), [Supreme Court summary](https://supremecourt.uk/cases/press-summary/uksc-2025-0042), [ALN Code](https://www.gov.wales/additional-learning-needs-code), [Welsh MHA Code](https://www.gov.wales/mental-health-act-1983-code-practice), current Social Care Wales Code/practice guidance, CPD changes and consolidation guidance were consulted. June 2026 liberty changes are now covered; current forms and case-specific authority still need checking in live practice.

The mental-health module distinguishes the Welsh Measure from the MHA and flags provision-specific commencement checks for the 2025 Act. ICO information-sharing guidance flags its own review after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025; the guide retains that qualification. Independent Welsh practitioner review remains separate and has not been claimed.
