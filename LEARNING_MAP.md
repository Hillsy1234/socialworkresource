# Learning Map

## The Big Picture

Social work decision-making usually sits inside five linked questions:

1. What does the person want, and what matters to them?
2. What needs, risks, strengths, rights, and protective factors are present?
3. Does the person have capacity for the specific decision, and what support have we provided?
4. What legal duty or power is engaged?
5. What is the least restrictive, rights-respecting, evidence-based plan?

## Legal Decision Map

Use this as an orientation tool, not as a substitute for local policy or legal advice.

- **Adult with possible care and support needs:** consider Care Act assessment, eligibility, prevention, information and advice, and advocacy.
- **Carer with possible support needs:** consider a Care Act carer's assessment and support planning.
- **Decision-specific capacity concern:** use the Mental Capacity Act. Support the person first, assess capacity for the specific decision, then move to best interests if needed.
- **Restrictions, supervision, locked doors, restraint, sedating medication, or not free in practice:** consider deprivation of liberty using the 2026 multifactorial approach. DoLS may apply in hospital or care home settings, while community arrangements may need Court of Protection authorisation.
- **Mental disorder, risk, need for hospital assessment or treatment, and possible compulsion:** consider the Mental Health Act assessment pathway.
- **Adult with care and support needs who is experiencing or at risk of abuse or neglect, and cannot protect themselves because of those needs:** consider the Care Act Section 42 safeguarding duty.
- **Child or young person under 18:** consider the Children Act, Working Together 2026, and local safeguarding arrangements.
- **Young person approaching adulthood:** consider transition assessment, the Care Act, Children Act, SEND, carers, and young carers.
- **Discrimination, dignity, liberty, family life, life, degrading treatment, participation, or reasonable adjustments:** include Equality Act and Human Rights Act analysis.

## Eight-Week Study Route

### Week 1: Foundations

Read:

- Professional Foundations
- Equality, Human Rights, and Recording

Practise:

- Write a one-page case formulation using facts, analysis, law, plan.
- Identify how the person's voice appears in your record.

### Week 2: Care Act

Read:

- Care Act 2014
- Quick Reference

Practise:

- Complete a mock Care Act assessment using the template.
- Identify needs, outcomes, eligibility, advocacy, carers, and review points.

### Week 3: MCA

Read:

- Mental Capacity Act 2005
- MCA templates in Templates and Checklists

Practise:

- Write a decision-specific capacity assessment.
- Convert a poor capacity statement into a strong evidence-led analysis.

### Week 4: DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty

Read:

- DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty

Practise:

- Screen a case using the 2026 multifactorial approach.
- Decide what evidence points toward or away from deprivation of liberty.

### Week 5: Mental Health Act

Read:

- Mental Health Act

Practise:

- Map the difference between MCA, DoLS, and MHA in a crisis scenario.
- Identify the AMHP's core analysis: risk, alternatives, least restriction, legal criteria, nearest relative or nominated person position.

### Week 6: Safeguarding Adults

Read:

- Safeguarding Adults

Practise:

- Make a Section 42 decision.
- Draft a Making Safeguarding Personal outcomes statement.

### Week 7: Children, Families, and Transition

Read:

- Children, Families, and Transitions

Practise:

- Identify whether a case is Section 17, Section 47, early help, SEND, transition, or adult safeguarding.

### Week 8: Consolidation

Read:

- Practice Scenarios
- Source Library

Practise:

- Pick three scenarios and write:
  - legal framework
  - strengths and risks
  - capacity/consent position
  - safeguarding position
  - proposed plan
  - what you would record

## Case Analysis Spine

Use this when stuck:

```text
1. Presenting issue
2. Person's own account, wishes, feelings, culture, and communication needs
3. Strengths, relationships, protective factors
4. Needs and impact on daily life
5. Risks: likelihood, severity, immediacy, pattern, triggers
6. Capacity and consent for each material decision
7. Legal frameworks engaged
8. Options considered, including less restrictive options
9. Professional analysis
10. Decision, rationale, plan, contingency, review
```

## Reflection Prompts

- What am I assuming, and what evidence supports it?
- What has the person said they want?
- Have I confused disagreement with incapacity?
- Have I confused compliance with consent?
- What would be the least restrictive plan that still manages the risk?
- Who has power in this situation, and how am I using mine?
- What would I need to evidence if this record were read in court?
