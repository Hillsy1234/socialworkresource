# 07 - Children, Families, and Transitions

## Purpose

Adult social workers still need a working knowledge of children, family, transition, young carer, SEND, and child safeguarding frameworks. Whole-family practice matters because adults rarely exist in isolation.

## Key Frameworks

- Children Act 1989
- Children Act 2004
- Children's Social Care National Framework 2026
- Working Together to Safeguard Children 2026
- Keeping Children Safe in Education
- Children and Families Act 2014
- Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 (phased commencement)
- SEND Code of Practice 0 to 25
- Care Act 2014 transition duties
- Domestic Abuse Act 2021

## Children Act: Basic Orientation

### Section 17

Section 17 concerns children in need. A child may be in need if they are unlikely to achieve or maintain a reasonable standard of health or development without services, their health or development is likely to be significantly impaired without services, or they are disabled.

### Section 47

Section 47 concerns child protection enquiries where there is reasonable cause to suspect a child is suffering or likely to suffer significant harm.

## Working Together 2026

Working Together is statutory guidance on multi-agency working to help, support, and protect children. It applies to organisations and agencies with functions relating to children and to education providers and childcare settings.

The 2026 update reinforces inclusive, anti-discriminatory practice, stronger information sharing, and a whole-family response. It describes Family Help as a more seamless offer combining targeted early help and section 17 support, led by a multi-disciplinary team and a family help plan where appropriate.

For adult social workers, the key message is: adult services are part of the child safeguarding system.

Think whole family where:

- an adult has mental ill health, substance use, domestic abuse, cognitive impairment, or care needs
- a child provides care
- a child is present during adult crisis interventions
- home conditions create risk
- an adult's risky relationships affect children
- a parent is detained under the Mental Health Act
- a person moving from children's to adults services has unmet needs

## Children's Social Care Reform Watch

The Children's Social Care National Framework is statutory guidance for local authority children's social care and relevant safeguarding partners. Use it alongside Working Together when considering purpose, practice principles, outcomes, and good-practice enablers.

The Children's Wellbeing and Schools Act 2026 is being implemented in stages. Check commencement separately for each provision; local policy does not determine whether a statutory duty is in force.

The information-sharing duty under section 16LA of the Children Act 2004 came into force in England on **30 September 2026**. Use the final [DfE statutory guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/information-sharing-to-safeguard-children-and-young-people), updated on 10 September, with its sharing-agreement templates. Establish whether the duty applies to your organisation, the information and the purpose; record the statutory conditions, lawful basis, recipient, necessary information and reasons for sharing or not sharing. The duty does not remove all confidentiality or data-protection safeguards. Do not assume other reforms, including every multi-agency child protection provision, commenced on the same date.

## Keeping Children Safe in Education

[KCSIE 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/keeping-children-safe-in-education--2) has been in force since **1 September 2026**, replacing the 2025 edition. All school and college staff should read Part One in full. The overview is complementary; the shortened Annex A version has been removed. These are education-sector expectations relevant to joint working, not a statement that all social workers are school staff.

Adult social workers may need this when working with schools, safeguarding partners, parents, young carers, or transition cases.

## Transition to Adulthood

Under the Care Act, transition planning should prevent young people and carers from falling between children's and adults services.

Consider transition assessment where there is likely need for adult care and support after turning 18 and where it would be of significant benefit to assess.

Transition work should cover:

- education, health, and care plans
- adult care eligibility
- mental capacity as the young person approaches 16 and 18
- parental responsibility changes
- carers and young carers
- housing
- employment, training, and occupation
- friendships and community
- health transition
- safeguarding
- finances and appointeeship
- advocacy

## SEND Code of Practice

The SEND Code applies to children and young people aged 0 to 25 in England. It is important for:

- Education, Health and Care plans
- disabled children and young people
- transition into adulthood
- social care advice into EHCP processes
- preparing for adulthood outcomes

Preparing for adulthood includes:

- employment or meaningful occupation
- independent living
- community inclusion
- health and wellbeing

## Young Carers

Young carers may support adults with illness, disability, mental health needs, substance use, or frailty.

Ask:

- What does the child do?
- Is the caring role age appropriate?
- Is school attendance affected?
- Is sleep, wellbeing, friendship, or safety affected?
- Is the child providing personal care, medication support, emotional containment, translation, or crisis support?
- Does the adult's care plan reduce inappropriate caring?
- Has a young carer's assessment been considered?

## Domestic Abuse and Children

Children can be victims of domestic abuse in their own right when they see, hear, or experience the effects of abuse and are related to either party.

Adult and children's safeguarding should be coordinated, not passed back and forth.

## Whole-Family Questions

- Who lives here and who stays here?
- Who depends on whom?
- Are there children, young carers, pregnant people, or vulnerable adults?
- What happens for the child when the adult is unwell, intoxicated, detained, missing, or in crisis?
- What is the adult willing for us to share, and is there a lawful basis to share without consent?
- What safety plan exists for evenings and weekends?

## Common Pitfalls

- Treating an adult case as if children are irrelevant.
- Assuming children's services know about the adult risk.
- Not considering young carers.
- Waiting until the 18th birthday to begin transition.
- Forgetting MCA applies from age 16.
- Confusing parental preference with the young person's wishes and rights.

## Disclosure and barring update — 22 October 2026

From 1 September 2026 the supervision exemption from regulated activity with children was removed in England and Wales. Supervision alone no longer excludes a role where the other regulated-activity criteria are met. Recheck affected staff and volunteer roles, the required enhanced DBS and barred-list checks, and referral duties against current guidance.
