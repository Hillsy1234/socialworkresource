# 04 - DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty

## Purpose

This module explains Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards, Court of Protection authorisation, and the changed legal position following the UK Supreme Court judgment on 2 June 2026.

## Current Position: July 2026

On 2 June 2026, the UK Supreme Court handed down judgment in `[2026] UKSC 16`, changing the approach to what counts as deprivation of liberty. DHSC published an update on 15 June 2026.

The previous Cheshire West 2014 "acid test" is no longer the single test. Practitioners must now use a multifactorial assessment.

DoLS has not disappeared. Where there is reason to believe a person is deprived of liberty:

- in a hospital or care home, the DoLS process still applies
- in community settings, Court of Protection authorisation may still be needed
- for children, legal advice may be needed, including High Court inherent jurisdiction or Court of Protection depending on age and circumstances

If in doubt, refer for DoLS or seek legal advice. DHSC has said a cautious approach is needed in borderline cases while the sector adjusts.

## What Changed in 2026

The old shorthand was:

```text
continuous supervision and control + not free to leave = deprivation of liberty
```

That is no longer enough on its own.

The current approach looks at the person's specific situation and considers multiple factors, including:

- type of restrictions
- duration of restrictions
- effect on the person
- manner of implementation
- whether the person objects
- whether the arrangements are closer to ordinary care or closer to detention
- relative normality of arrangements in the setting
- purpose of the restrictions, such as care and protection rather than punishment or coercion
- the person's wishes and feelings
- whether the person can validly consent through expression of wishes and feelings

No single factor decides the answer.

## Compliance Is Not Consent

Do not assume consent because the person is quiet, passive, polite, institutionalised, sedated, fearful, or used to restrictions.

Ask:

- How do we know what the person understands?
- How do we know what the person wants?
- Can the person express acceptance or objection?
- Are they under pressure?
- Are they afraid of consequences?
- Is medication, pain, fatigue, trauma, or communication difficulty affecting expression?
- Have we heard the person's wishes away from people who may influence them?

## Objection

Objection can be verbal or non-verbal. It may include:

- saying they want to leave
- repeated attempts to leave
- distress at locked doors
- refusing care linked to confinement
- resisting return to placement
- calling family, police, or services to get out
- agitation, withdrawal, or behavioural distress connected to restrictions
- objecting to medication, treatment, residence, or care arrangements

Any objection points toward possible deprivation of liberty and away from valid consent.

## When to Consider DoLS

Consider DoLS where the person is in a hospital or care home and:

- they lack capacity for residence/care/treatment arrangements
- restrictions may amount to deprivation of liberty after multifactorial analysis
- the arrangements are necessary to prevent harm
- the restrictions are significant or continuous
- the person objects or fluctuates between contentment and objection
- there are locked doors, keypad exits, 1:1 supervision, restraint, bedrails, sedating medication, restrictions on visitors, or controlled access to the community
- there is doubt about valid consent

## DoLS Assessments

The six qualifying assessments are:

1. Age assessment.
2. Mental health assessment.
3. Mental capacity assessment.
4. Best interests assessment.
5. Eligibility assessment.
6. No refusals assessment.

The Best Interests Assessor considers whether the deprivation is:

- occurring or likely to occur
- in the person's best interests
- necessary to prevent harm
- proportionate to likelihood and seriousness of harm
- the least restrictive available option

The BIA may recommend conditions and duration.

## Representatives and Rights

A person subject to DoLS must have safeguards, which may include:

- Relevant Person's Representative
- IMCA where required
- rights to review
- rights to challenge in the Court of Protection
- clear authorisation period
- conditions
- ongoing care planning and review

## Community Deprivation of Liberty

DoLS does not apply in supported living, shared lives, ordinary homes, extra care, or community placements. If arrangements may amount to deprivation of liberty in those settings, Court of Protection authorisation may be required.

Examples:

- locked supported living placement
- continuous supervision in own home
- restrictions on leaving or contact
- forced return from family visits
- significant restraint or sedation in the community

After the 2026 judgment, use the multifactorial assessment first. If Article 5 may still be engaged, seek legal advice.

## DoLS, Care Act, and MCA

DoLS authorises deprivation of liberty. It does not replace:

- Care Act assessment and care planning
- MCA capacity and best interests decision-making
- safeguarding duties
- CQC and provider responsibilities
- professional duties to reduce restrictions
- review of medication and restraint

## DoLS and Mental Health Act

Consider the Mental Health Act where:

- the person appears to need hospital assessment or treatment for mental disorder
- detention for mental disorder may be necessary
- the person is objecting to admission or treatment for mental disorder
- DoLS eligibility may be affected

This interface is complex. Use AMHP advice, legal advice, and local policy.

## Common Pitfalls

- Applying the old acid test as if nothing changed.
- Assuming no DoLS is needed because the person is not trying to leave.
- Assuming DoLS is needed for every restrictive care plan without multifactorial analysis.
- Confusing physical inability to leave with state-imposed confinement.
- Forgetting to review existing authorisations.
- Treating authorisation as permission to stop thinking about least restriction.
- Failing to record the person's wishes and feelings in detail.
