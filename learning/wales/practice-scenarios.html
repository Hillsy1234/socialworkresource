Wales scenario workouts

All eight cases are fictional. Write a response to the questions before revealing the discussion. Several legal and practice routes may apply at once.

Work through a case

  1. Separate what is known, reported and assumed.
  2. Identify the person’s wishes, language and participation needs.
  3. Explain which duties or processes need attention and what is uncertain.
  4. Compare your reasoning with the discussion, then open the linked modules.
  5. Record one learning action and a source to check in supervision.

The discussion gives a possible reasoning route, not a definitive case decision.

Worked cases

Care and carer support

Elin, 79, has fallen twice. Her daughter, who works nights, says she cannot continue daily visits. Elin wants to remain at home.

  • Whose assessment is needed and what matters to each person?
  • What support is available without assuming the daughter will provide it?
  • How will you distinguish assessment, eligibility and charging?

Discussion: Explore Elin’s outcomes, daily living, strengths and barriers, and assess the daughter’s own support needs. Discuss willingness and sustainability separately from affection. Compare home support, adaptations and other options with Elin; identify immediate support, responsible people and a review date. Financial circumstances do not replace the needs assessment.

Capacity and an unwise decision

Sian declines a proposed move after a tiring hospital stay. Staff say the risk means she lacks capacity. She has asked to speak Welsh.

  • What is the exact decision and why is capacity in doubt?
  • What practical support must come first?
  • What evidence would distinguish disagreement from inability to decide?

Discussion: Arrange suitable timing, language and accessible information. Record Sian’s reasoning and how she uses or weighs the relevant options. Risk alone does not establish incapacity. If the statutory test is met, identify the decision-maker and a separate best interests process; assess any liberty issue separately.

Liberty and the person’s wishes

Carys lives in a care home with locked doors and staff accompanying trips. Sometimes she asks to leave; the record says she is settled.

  • What restrictions actually operate?
  • How will you explore wishes, objection and consent?
  • Which current Welsh advice and authorisation route needs checking?

Discussion: Describe duration, intensity, freedom to leave, support and the person’s account. Apply current law following the June 2026 judgment, not the former acid test alone. Do not equate quiet behaviour with valid consent or incapacity with lack of consent for every purpose. Seek the appropriate DoLS or legal advice and record review and challenge arrangements.

Mental health crisis

Owain is distressed in an emergency department and wants to leave. The team is considering compulsory assessment. Physical treatment is also proposed.

  • What is the immediate risk and purpose of each proposed intervention?
  • Who must assess the mental health legal route?
  • How will you address physical treatment and communication separately?

Discussion: Involve the clinical team and AMHP through the local pathway. Identify the lawful authority for each intervention and available less restrictive options. Use the Welsh MHA Code and check the current provision and commencement position. A capacity assessment is not a general power to detain; physical treatment may involve a different legal analysis.

Adult at risk and financial control

Rhys has care and support needs. His relative controls his bank card and answers all questions. Rhys says there is no problem while the relative is beside him.

  • Do the facts give reasonable cause to suspect an adult at risk under section 126?
  • How can Rhys be heard safely and privately?
  • Which immediate reporting and protection steps are needed?

Discussion: Consider abuse or neglect risk, care and support needs and ability to protect himself because of those needs. Follow Welsh reporting procedures, relevant section 128 duties and the local authority enquiry route. Explore coercion, advocacy and desired outcomes. Do not delay reporting while seeking proof, or treat apparent agreement as resolving the concern.

Transition and an IDP

Seren is 17, has an individual development plan and wants to attend college away from home. Her parent provides substantial daily support.

  • What does Seren want and how will she participate?
  • Who is coordinating education, health, care and family support?
  • Which decisions and services need planning before adulthood?

Discussion: Bring together Welsh care and support assessment, ALN/IDP planning, health and carer support. Name owners and dates for actions and clarify which decisions use the MCA from age 16. An IDP does not settle all social care duties. Plan continuity and disagreement routes instead of relying on an age-bound handover.

Organisational safeguarding

Several care-home residents have unexplained injuries and staff describe routine restrictions as policy. Records are inconsistent.

  • Which immediate individual protection steps are required?
  • What pattern needs a coordinated safeguarding response?
  • Which information and agencies must be involved?

Discussion: Attend to each adult’s safety, wishes and needs while following Welsh safeguarding procedures. Preserve factual records, report concerns and consider police, commissioners and Care Inspectorate Wales as appropriate. Check individual capacity and liberty arrangements rather than treating a provider policy as legal authority.

Language and professional disagreement

A Welsh-speaking carer says meetings are inaccessible. A draft plan records agreement, but the carer reports feeling pressured.

  • Whose account appears in the plan?
  • What changes would make the meeting accessible?
  • How will disagreement and the next review be recorded?

Discussion: Speak directly with the carer using appropriate language support. Correct the record so that agreement, disagreement and professional analysis are distinct. Explore support needs and advocacy. Escalate unresolved access barriers and record who will act and how the carer can challenge the plan.
