Mental Health in Wales

Learning outcomes

Distinguish compulsory powers from care planning, recognise Wales-specific mental-health sources, and structure an interface discussion involving capacity, liberty, safety and support.

Start with the Welsh Code

The Mental Health Act 1983, as amended, provides compulsory assessment and treatment routes. Wales has its own Code of Practice. Use the current statutory criteria and safeguards for the particular section; a diagnosis or disagreement with professionals is not a substitute for those criteria. The Code addresses participation, lawful decision-making, least restriction and professional responsibilities. Welsh Code of Practice.

Recognise the purpose of each route

Route to investigateLearning distinction
Section 2Admission for assessment, or assessment followed by treatment; check current criteria and time limits.
Section 3Admission for treatment, subject to the applicable statutory conditions.
Emergency and holding powersSpecific powers with limited scope; they are not general substitutes for assessment or longer-term authority.
Community treatment or guardianshipDifferent powers, conditions and limits; do not treat them as blanket authority over everyday decisions.
Section 117Aftercare duties for qualifying patients; identify the statutory basis and responsible health/local-authority bodies.

An AMHP brings independent social analysis to the statutory process. Explore social circumstances, alternatives, consultation, the person's account and the practicality of support. Decisions should explain both why intervention is proposed and why less restrictive options would or would not work. Read the section in the current Act and the relevant Code chapter before applying it.

Wales care coordination and the Measure

The Mental Health (Wales) Measure 2010 includes primary mental-health support, coordination and planning for secondary mental-health service users, assessment routes for eligible former users, and advocacy provisions. Care and Treatment Plans are part of the Welsh framework; they are not detention authorisations. Senedd research overview.

For someone leaving services, clarify the named contact, relapse indicators, support arrangements and how to request help again. Welsh Government describes an assessment route for eligible former secondary-service users under Part 3. Check eligibility, residence and the applicable period rather than assuming every former patient qualifies. Part 3 information.

Capacity, liberty and treatment

Separate the decision about hospital assessment or treatment from decisions about residence, physical healthcare, finances and everyday care. Ask which legal framework addresses each decision, who can make it and what authority is actually in force. Capacity and consent still matter; neither MCA nor DoLS should be treated as a convenient substitute for considering the correct mental-health pathway. Seek specialist advice where the regimes interact or the person objects.

Reform and commencement

The Mental Health Act 2025 received Royal Assent in December 2025. Royal Assent does not mean every reform is operational. For any proposed change, check the relevant provision, commencement instrument, territory, current forms and local implementation. This guide does not assume all reforms have commenced. Government reform collection.

Worked learning example

Fictional example: Owain is distressed in hospital and wants to leave. Describe immediate concerns without deciding the legal route from distress alone. Clarify the purpose of any proposed restriction, clinical and social information, communication support, alternatives, current legal status and the appropriate professional to assess. Record who is responsible for the next decision and review; “awaiting assessment” should not conceal an unexplained restriction.
