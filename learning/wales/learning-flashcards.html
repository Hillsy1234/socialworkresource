Wales learning flashcards

Forty cards across eight modules. Recall an answer before flipping the card, then explain how it would affect a fictional case. Use the matching module and its official sources to check the detail. Cards are revision prompts, not statutory decision tests.

Revision cards

Professional Foundations: Which professional guidance applies in Wales?

The current Social Care Wales Code and social-worker practice guidance. Check the version and effective date.

Professional Foundations: What makes a formulation more than a list of events?

It separates evidence, the person’s perspective, possible explanations and a reasoned plan with a review point.

Professional Foundations: How do you test a strengths-based plan?

Ask whether the proposed support is available, wanted and sustainable; do not presume family or community support.

Professional Foundations: What does professional curiosity add?

Questions about discrepancies and alternative explanations, tested respectfully against evidence and the person’s account.

Professional Foundations: What should a reflection lead to?

A specific change, feedback or further enquiry and a way to review its impact in supervision.

Care and Support in Wales: What is the main Welsh care and support framework?

The Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014, with the relevant regulations and Welsh codes.

Care and Support in Wales: Which assessment sections distinguish adults, children and carers?

Sections 19, 21 and 24 respectively. Read the applicable criteria and Part 3 Code for the actual duty.

Care and Support in Wales: Does a needs assessment establish what someone will pay?

No. Assessment, eligibility, meeting needs and charging are distinct stages with their own rules.

Care and Support in Wales: Can willingness to care be assumed from a family relationship?

No. Explore the carer’s own outcomes, ability and willingness, and the sustainability of proposed care.

Care and Support in Wales: What does advocacy planning require?

Identify barriers to participation, available support and whether independent professional advocacy is required under the Welsh framework.

Mental Capacity Act 2005: What decision does a capacity assessment address?

A specific decision at a particular time, after all practicable support; not global capacity.

Mental Capacity Act 2005: Does a risky refusal establish incapacity?

No. Examine the decision-making process and statutory test, rather than whether professionals approve the outcome.

Mental Capacity Act 2005: What are the functional abilities?

Understanding, retaining, using or weighing relevant information, and communicating a decision by any means.

Mental Capacity Act 2005: What causal connection matters?

An inability to make the decision must result from an impairment of, or disturbance in, the functioning of mind or brain.

Mental Capacity Act 2005: Does a best interests decision itself authorise deprivation of liberty?

No. Identify the appropriate separate authority where the arrangements amount to deprivation of liberty.

DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty: What changed in June 2026?

The Supreme Court departed from Cheshire West. Use the current judgment and Welsh guidance rather than the former acid test alone.

DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty: Is silence the same as valid consent?

No. Explore the person’s wishes and actual situation; mere acquiescence is insufficient.

DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty: Is Article 5 consent simply the same question as domestic MCA capacity?

No. The June 2026 judgment distinguishes these questions. Seek appropriate advice when applying it.

DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty: What must a restriction review describe?

The actual arrangements, setting, duration, intensity, effects, freedom to leave and the person’s account and objection.

DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty: Which settings are central to the DoLS administrative route?

Hospitals and care homes for adults aged 18 or over, subject to the statutory requirements. Other settings or ages need the appropriate legal route.

Mental Health in Wales: Which MHA Code applies to Wales?

The Welsh Code, read with the current Act, amendments and commencement information.

Mental Health in Wales: What distinguishes section 2 from section 3 in learning terms?

Section 2 concerns admission for assessment, potentially followed by treatment; section 3 concerns admission for treatment. Check all current statutory criteria.

Mental Health in Wales: Why separate the purpose of proposed interventions?

Mental health compulsion, physical treatment, care and accommodation may require different legal analyses and authorities.

Mental Health in Wales: What Welsh framework supports care coordination in secondary mental health services?

Part 2 of the Mental Health (Wales) Measure 2010 provides the relevant care coordination and care/treatment planning framework.

Mental Health in Wales: Does enactment of reform mean every provision is in force?

No. Check commencement and territorial application for the specific provision and date of the decision.

Safeguarding Adults in Wales: What are the three adult-at-risk elements in section 126?

Abuse or neglect, or risk of it; care and support needs; and inability to protect oneself as a result of those needs.

Safeguarding Adults in Wales: Where is the Welsh local authority adult enquiry duty?

Section 126(2), where the authority has reasonable cause to suspect an adult in its area is an adult at risk.

Safeguarding Adults in Wales: What does section 128 concern?

Relevant partners’ duty to inform the local authority where the statutory adult-at-risk reporting conditions apply.

Safeguarding Adults in Wales: Must abuse be proved before reporting?

No. Follow the reporting threshold and Welsh procedures; do not delay a concern to conduct your own proof-gathering exercise.

Safeguarding Adults in Wales: How should apparent refusal under coercion be approached?

Arrange safe communication, explore the person’s account, capacity and coercion, and consider advocacy and lawful protective action.

Children, Families and Transitions: Which safeguarding procedure collection applies?

Wales Safeguarding Procedures and Welsh statutory guidance, alongside the applicable legislation.

Children, Families and Transitions: What education plan terminology is used in Wales?

Additional learning needs (ALN) and individual development plans (IDPs). Check duties for the individual learner.

Children, Families and Transitions: What should transition planning connect?

The young person’s wishes, education, health, care and support, housing, relationships, family support and continuity of services.

Children, Families and Transitions: Does an IDP decide all social care responsibilities?

No. Education planning and care/support duties need coordinated but distinct consideration.

Children, Families and Transitions: What makes a child-centred record?

The child’s own account and daily lived experience, supported communication, evidence, analysis and a plan they can understand.

Rights, Language and Recording: What is an active offer of Welsh-language support?

Proactively making Welsh-language support available and arranging how the preference will be met, rather than waiting for repeated requests.

Rights, Language and Recording: What should a reasonable adjustment record show?

The barrier, adjustment offered, whether it worked and what further action or review is needed.

Rights, Language and Recording: Is consent always necessary for safeguarding information sharing?

No. Establish the lawful basis and justification, share relevant information proportionately and record the reasons.

Rights, Language and Recording: How should disagreement appear in a record?

Identify who disagrees and why, separate views from evidence and analysis, and record response, escalation and review.

Rights, Language and Recording: What makes a rights analysis practical?

Identify the right engaged, proposed interference, legal basis, alternatives and proportionality, then record the decision and review.
