The cases are fictional and the discussions are learning examples. Establish the California source and local route for any legal conclusion. Consider what evidence is missing, alternative explanations, responsibility for next actions and how outcomes will be reviewed.

Worked cases

Care and family support

An older adult wants to remain at home after two falls. Their daughter works nights and cannot sustain daily visits.

  • Which needs and outcomes belong to the person and to carers?
  • Which service actually assesses and funds this support?
  • What happens if the proposed support is unavailable?

Discussion: Separate the person’s needs from the daughter’s circumstances. Explore available services and practical changes, agree interim safety measures and name who will check whether the plan works. Use the California module and source library to identify the applicable framework.

A decision and a refusal

After a tiring hospital stay, a person refuses a move. Staff interpret the refusal as inability to decide; the person requests an interpreter.

  • What is the precise decision and why is ability to decide questioned?
  • What communication support would help?
  • Who has authority for this decision under the applicable law?

Discussion: Arrange language support and suitable timing. Identify the decision and applicable legal criteria. Record the person’s reasoning, check any representative’s authority and distinguish a capacity question from disagreement. Use the California module and source library to identify the applicable framework.

Restrictions and objections

A resident lives behind locked doors and is accompanied on outings. The record says they are settled, although they sometimes ask to leave.

  • What restrictions actually operate and how do they affect the person?
  • What authority is being relied upon?
  • How can the person challenge the arrangements?

Discussion: Describe the actual restrictions and hear the person’s account. Examine consent, authority and alternatives through the relevant local legal route. Record objections, independent support and review. Use the California module and source library to identify the applicable framework.

Mental-health crisis

A distressed person in an emergency department wants to leave. Compulsory assessment and physical treatment are both proposed.

  • What is the purpose of each proposed intervention?
  • Who can assess or exercise the relevant power?
  • What voluntary or less restrictive support is available?

Discussion: Clarify immediate needs and the purpose of each intervention. Involve authorised professionals through the local pathway, explain rights and identify separate legal bases where needed. Use the California module and source library to identify the applicable framework.

Financial control

A relative holds a person’s bank card and answers every question. The person says everything is fine while the relative is present.

  • How can the person be heard safely and privately?
  • Which reporting pathway applies to this person and setting?
  • What immediate action and follow-up are required?

Discussion: Offer safe private communication. Explore coercion, the person’s wishes and immediate safety. Identify reporting requirements and proportionate protective options without assuming a relative has financial authority. Moving into adult services

A 17-year-old with education and daily-living support wants to attend college away from home. Several services expect another team to coordinate the move.

  • What does the child say or show about safety and daily life?
  • Which reporting duty and receiving agency apply?
  • How will education, health and family support coordinate?

Discussion: Ask the young person about goals and support preferences. Map the local education and adult-service processes, confirm age-specific requirements, assign responsibilities and plan continuity rather than relying on a birthday handover. Organisational concerns

Several residents have unexplained injuries. Staff describe rushed care, and earlier concerns have not received a response.

  • How can the person be heard safely and privately?
  • Which reporting pathway applies to this person and setting?
  • What immediate action and follow-up are required?

Discussion: Address immediate safety, preserve relevant evidence and identify the correct safeguarding, regulatory and employer routes. Confirm who will respond and escalate unresolved concerns while protecting residents’ participation. Use the California module and source library to identify the applicable framework.

Communication and agreement

A carer reports that meetings are inaccessible and that they felt pressured to agree to a plan. The record states that everyone agreed.

  • What makes participation accessible for this person?
  • What legal basis permits disclosure of this information?
  • How will disagreement and reasons be recorded?

Discussion: Arrange accessible communication and revisit the person’s account. Correct the record through the proper process, explain choices and complaints routes, and review whether the plan is voluntary and workable.
