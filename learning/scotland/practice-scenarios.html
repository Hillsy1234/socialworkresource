Scenario Workouts — Scotland practice guide

All cases are fictional learning exercises. Explain your evidence and reasoning before reading the discussion.

Professional Foundations — Scotland

A worker is asked to close a referral because a family has missed two appointments. The person says bus fares and caring duties prevented attendance.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Test the access barrier before concluding that support is unwanted. Offer a workable contact method, clarify the person’s outcomes and record the referral decision and its reasons. In supervision, distinguish a resource pressure from evidence about need and willingness.

Social Care and Support — Scotland

An adult wants assistance to attend evening activities, while a parent asks the authority to arrange all daytime support.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Explore the adult’s outcomes and whether a combination of SDS options would fit. Establish what the parent is willing to do and any need for a separate carer plan. Confirm actual evening provision, management support and costs before describing the package as agreed.

Decision-Making and Capacity — Scotland

A man can choose his meals and visitors but struggles to understand a proposed sale of his home. His sister holds a welfare power of attorney.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Keep the property decision distinct from daily choices and welfare matters. Provide accessible financial information and check the document’s powers. Do not let the welfare appointment stand in for financial authority. Explain which advice or court process is needed if the sale remains necessary and authority is missing.

Restrictions and Liberty — Scotland

A supported-living service locks the front door at night and says a guardian agreed to the placement. The adult repeatedly asks to leave.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Obtain the order and distinguish placement powers from the measures staff enforce. Record the adult’s wishes, actual ability to leave and alternatives such as staffing or environmental changes. Escalate the legal basis of confinement promptly and arrange support to challenge it; family approval is insufficient reasoning.

Mental Health — Scotland

A person experiencing severe mental distress declines admission but accepts daily support and an urgent medical review at home.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Assess whether the proposed community support is real and sufficient, and compare it with the relevant statutory criteria. Record the person’s reasoning and the MHO’s assessment. Do not substitute disagreement with the recommended treatment for evidence of significantly impaired decision-making ability or necessity.

Adult Safeguarding — Scotland

A 61-year-old understands their bank account but depends on a neighbour who takes their money and controls access to shopping.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Do not reject the concern because the adult can explain the transactions. Explore dependency, coercion and ability to safeguard their interests against the three criteria. Arrange a safe conversation, practical access to essentials and the appropriate council and police response.

Children, Families and Transitions — Scotland

A 15-year-old misses school to support an ill parent and says they want to stay at home but need help.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Explore the young person’s safety, education and caring responsibilities. Coordinate support and consider a young carer statement. Establish whether there is evidence requiring a protection or Reporter route, while avoiding a conclusion that school absence alone settles that question.

Rights, Culture and Recording — Scotland

A deaf parent is offered only telephone appointments and is then described as failing to engage with a family assessment.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Arrange suitable communication support, correct the inaccurate inference and reassess the effect of the service barrier. Record the parent’s preferred method, the adjustment agreed and responsibility for providing it. Review the assessment outcome in light of the earlier exclusion.
