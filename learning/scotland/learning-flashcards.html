Learning Flashcards — Scotland

Recall and explain

The eight decks contain five cards each. Choose a module, answer in your own words, then reveal the answer. Use the linked module and source library for legal detail; a short card cannot reproduce a statutory test.

Review difficult cards

Explain the distinction with a fictional example. Record uncertainty for supervision and return to the card after reading the source. The cards test learning and do not certify competence.

Revision cards

Professional Foundations — Scotland: Where should you begin checking professional foundations in Scotland?

Start with Scottish Social Services Council (SSSC). Check the professional requirements for your role and the applicable law.

Professional Foundations — Scotland: How does a formulation differ from a chronology?

It connects evidence, the person’s perspective and possible explanations to a reasoned plan.

Professional Foundations — Scotland: How can supervision test bias?

Ask what evidence challenges your preferred explanation and whose perspective is missing.

Professional Foundations — Scotland: What makes a professional record useful?

It distinguishes observations, reported information, analysis, actions and review responsibilities.

Professional Foundations — Scotland: What makes reflective learning actionable?

A specific change to practice, a way to seek feedback and a date to revisit its impact.

Social Care and Support — Scotland: Where should you begin checking social care and support in Scotland?

Open Scottish social-work legislation register and Self-directed Support statutory guidance from the module. Confirm scope, version and local procedures.

Social Care and Support — Scotland: Can family support be assumed?

No. Explore willingness, ability and sustainability with each person involved.

Social Care and Support — Scotland: Why distinguish assessment from funding?

A description of need does not itself determine eligibility, availability or costs under a particular programme.

Social Care and Support — Scotland: What belongs in a support plan?

Desired outcomes, agreed actions, responsible people, contingency and review.

Social Care and Support — Scotland: What if support is unavailable?

Record the unmet need, interim arrangements and escalation; do not record an unavailable service as an agreed solution.

Decision-Making and Capacity — Scotland: Where should you begin checking decision-making and capacity in Scotland?

Open Adults with Incapacity (Scotland) Act guidance and Adult Support and Protection capacity guidance from the module. Confirm scope, version and local procedures.

Decision-Making and Capacity — Scotland: Does a risky refusal alone establish inability to decide?

No. Explore the decision-making process, supports and applicable legal criteria.

Decision-Making and Capacity — Scotland: What support can improve participation?

Accessible information, suitable timing, interpretation and communication aids chosen for the person.

Decision-Making and Capacity — Scotland: Does a family relationship establish decision-making authority?

Do not assume it does. Verify the applicable law and any appointment or authorisation.

Decision-Making and Capacity — Scotland: Why identify the exact decision?

Different decisions can involve different abilities, legal tests and representatives. Avoid a global label.

Restrictions and Liberty — Scotland: Where should you begin checking restrictions and liberty in Scotland?

Open Adults with Incapacity Scotland and Mental Welfare Commission from the module. Confirm scope, version and local procedures.

Restrictions and Liberty — Scotland: What should a restriction record describe?

Actual arrangements, duration, intensity, effects, ability to leave and the person’s account.

Restrictions and Liberty — Scotland: Does quiet behaviour demonstrate consent?

Do not assume consent from silence. Explore wishes, understanding, pressure and communication.

Restrictions and Liberty — Scotland: Can provider policy alone establish legal authority?

A policy must operate within applicable law. Identify the legal basis and scope of the proposed restriction.

Restrictions and Liberty — Scotland: What should review consider?

Whether the restriction remains justified, less restrictive alternatives, objections and routes to challenge.

Mental Health — Scotland: Where should you begin checking mental health in Scotland?

Open Scottish mental-health law and guidance and Mental Welfare Commission from the module. Confirm scope, version and local procedures.

Mental Health — Scotland: Why distinguish admission from treatment?

The legal basis for admission may differ from the authority to provide a particular treatment.

Mental Health — Scotland: What should happen alongside crisis assessment?

Attend to immediate health needs, communication, the person’s account and available support.

Mental Health — Scotland: Can a social worker use every mental-health power?

No. Establish the qualifications, appointment and authority required for the particular role.

Mental Health — Scotland: What does a rights explanation cover?

The proposed action, reasons, available advocacy, review and ways to raise concerns.

Adult Safeguarding — Scotland: Where should you begin checking adult safeguarding in Scotland?

Open Scottish Government adult support and protection overview and 2022 ASP Code of Practice from the module. Confirm scope, version and local procedures.

Adult Safeguarding — Scotland: Why offer a private conversation?

It may help identify coercion or harm that a person cannot safely describe in front of others.

Adult Safeguarding — Scotland: Is proof needed before checking reporting duties?

Do not wait to prove abuse. Consider the applicable reporting threshold and act promptly when it is met.

Adult Safeguarding — Scotland: What should a report contain?

Relevant facts, the concern, immediate safety needs, the person’s account and actions already taken.

Adult Safeguarding — Scotland: Does a referral complete the response?

No. Confirm receipt, identify responsibility, review safety and escalate unresolved concerns as needed.

Children, Families and Transitions — Scotland: Where should you begin checking children, families and transitions in Scotland?

Open Children’s Hearings Scotland and Scottish Government children’s hearings guidance from the module. Confirm scope, version and local procedures.

Children, Families and Transitions — Scotland: How should the child’s account be recorded?

Use their own words where possible and distinguish what they said from adult interpretation.

Children, Families and Transitions — Scotland: Can one child-protection model settle a legal threshold?

No. A practice model helps enquiry; reporting and intervention require the applicable legal analysis.

Children, Families and Transitions — Scotland: What makes transition planning useful?

Named responsibilities and dates across services, guided by the young person’s goals and continuity of support.

Children, Families and Transitions — Scotland: Why consider community and cultural relationships?

They may support identity, participation and safety. Ask the family rather than making assumptions.

Rights, Culture and Recording — Scotland: Where should you begin checking rights, culture and recording in Scotland?

Open SSSC Codes and National Care Service Charter and law from the module. Confirm scope, version and local procedures.

Rights, Culture and Recording — Scotland: What is data minimisation?

Sharing only the information needed for the identified, lawful purpose.

Rights, Culture and Recording — Scotland: What is a culturally responsive first step?

Ask the person how they want to communicate and which cultural or community supports they want involved.

Rights, Culture and Recording — Scotland: Why record disagreement?

It preserves the person’s perspective and makes professional reasoning and review more accountable.

Rights, Culture and Recording — Scotland: How do you compare alternatives?

Consider likely benefit, burdens, rights, the person’s preferences and whether a less intrusive option could work.
