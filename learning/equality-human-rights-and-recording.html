08 - Equality, Human Rights, and Recording

Purpose

This module helps keep social work lawful, anti-oppressive, and defensible.

Equality Act 2010

Protected characteristics include:

  • age
  • disability
  • gender reassignment
  • marriage and civil partnership
  • pregnancy and maternity
  • race
  • religion or belief
  • sex
  • sexual orientation

Practice duties include:

  • making reasonable adjustments
  • avoiding direct and indirect discrimination
  • recognising harassment and victimisation
  • considering the Public Sector Equality Duty where relevant

Reasonable Adjustments

Adjustments may include:

  • accessible formats
  • interpreter
  • communication aids
  • extra time
  • quiet environment
  • home visit
  • involving a trusted person
  • flexible appointments
  • advocacy
  • sensory adaptations
  • accessible transport

Record adjustments offered and whether they worked.

Human Rights Act

Commonly relevant rights:

  • Article 2: right to life
  • Article 3: freedom from torture, inhuman or degrading treatment
  • Article 5: liberty and security
  • Article 6: fair process
  • Article 8: private and family life, home, and correspondence
  • Article 14: non-discrimination in enjoyment of rights

Human rights analysis is not abstract. It helps answer:

  • What right is engaged?
  • Is interference lawful?
  • Is there a legitimate aim?
  • Is the action necessary and proportionate?
  • Is there a less restrictive alternative?
  • Has the person been involved?
  • Are safeguards available?

Anti-Oppressive Practice

Ask:

  • How might racism, ableism, sexism, homophobia, transphobia, ageism, poverty, immigration status, trauma, or institutional mistrust shape this situation?
  • Whose account is being believed most easily?
  • Is the person being described in loaded language?
  • Are services expecting the person to adapt to the system rather than adapting the system to the person?
  • Is risk being overestimated because of stigma or underestimated because of familiarity?

Culturally Responsive Practice

Do:

  • ask, do not assume
  • use interpreters
  • recognise religious and cultural networks as possible strengths
  • explore family roles without stereotyping
  • understand migration, racism, asylum, and trauma contexts
  • consider food, prayer, gender, modesty, grief, and end-of-life customs
  • challenge harmful practice without condemning identity

Good Recording

Good records are:

  • accurate
  • timely
  • respectful
  • evidence-led
  • analytical
  • clear about legal basis
  • clear about the person's voice
  • clear about disagreement
  • clear about risk and protective factors
  • clear about decisions and review

Recording Formula

What happened?
Who said what?
What did I observe?
What evidence supports this?
What does the person want?
What law/policy applies?
What options were considered?
What decision was made?
Why is it proportionate?
What happens next?

Language to Avoid

Avoid:

  • non-compliant
  • attention-seeking
  • manipulative
  • difficult family
  • refusing to engage
  • lifestyle choice
  • bed blocker
  • failed placement
  • challenging behaviour without context

Use:

  • declined
  • distressed
  • disagreed
  • unable to attend
  • communication of unmet need
  • pattern of self-neglect
  • family disputes the plan
  • placement is no longer meeting needs

Defensible Decision-Making

A decision is more defensible when it shows:

  • relevant law and policy were considered
  • relevant information was gathered
  • the person was involved
  • capacity and consent were addressed
  • risks and benefits were balanced
  • less restrictive options were considered
  • discrimination and human rights were considered
  • reasons were recorded
  • review or contingency was set

Common Pitfalls

  • Recording professional opinion as fact.
  • Omitting the person's own words.
  • Describing behaviour without context.
  • Using vague risk phrases.
  • Copying forward old assessments.
  • Not explaining why options were rejected.
  • Forgetting equality and communication needs.
  • Recording "family agreed" without saying who, what authority they had, and whether the person agreed.
