Scenario Workouts — British Columbia practice guide

All cases are fictional learning exercises. Explain your evidence and reasoning before reading the discussion.

Professional Foundations — British Columbia

A worker assumes that employment by a health authority automatically gives them the powers of a designated adult-protection investigator.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Check the organisation’s designation and the worker’s actual role, authority and procedure. Gather relevant social evidence within scope and arrange the appropriate designated-agency response. Employment and statutory investigative authority should not be conflated.

Social Care and Support — British Columbia

A recently arrived older adult is told that a general residence requirement means no assessment or alternative support can be considered.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Check the published exceptions and the person’s actual health needs and circumstances. Identify any other relevant service while the eligibility question is resolved. Record reasons, interim support and the route for challenging the decision.

Decision-Making and Capacity — British Columbia

A friend offers to consent to treatment because they visit most often, although a valid representation agreement may appoint somebody else.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Assess the adult’s own ability and read the agreement. Determine the appropriate healthcare-consent route and priority before seeking consent. Frequent contact is useful evidence about wishes but does not by itself establish authority.

Restrictions and Liberty — British Columbia

A care facility locks a resident’s door after an adult-protection referral and says the referral gives it authority to continue indefinitely.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Clarify whether any lawful emergency or court measure exists and its limits. Review the actual restriction and the adult’s wishes, arrange safer alternatives and obtain the appropriate legal or designated-agency advice. A referral itself confers no indefinite detention power.

Mental Health — British Columbia

A person is detained and a worker assumes the treatment-consent process must be identical to the one used in a neighbouring province.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Read the BC provisions governing the person’s actual status and proposed treatment. Explain the specific review and second-opinion rights, and assess unrelated physical healthcare under its own framework. Avoid substituting a familiar out-of-province process.

Adult Safeguarding — British Columbia

A person understands that a family member is taking money but is physically prevented from leaving home or contacting services.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Consider the inability to seek assistance and the relevant designated-agency route rather than rejecting the concern because the person understands the abuse. Arrange safe communication, practical protection and any police response, with the adult’s wishes clearly recorded.

Children, Families and Transitions — British Columbia

A worker hears a serious concern about an 18-year-old and dismisses it because the worker previously practised where adult services began earlier.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Check the BC age definition and applicable protection conditions. Make the appropriate report and coordinate support with the young person’s participation. Identify any relevant Indigenous authority and transition arrangements rather than importing the former jurisdiction’s boundary.

Rights, Culture and Recording — British Columbia

A private community provider copies a public health authority’s privacy form and assumes that the same disclosure powers apply.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Determine the provider’s actual legal obligations and purpose of sharing. Revise the consent and information process to fit the applicable law, and record what the person was told. Similar work does not establish identical statutory powers.
