Children and Family Practice Models — Ontario

Compare approaches to children and families

Use the model finder to explore enquiry, family relationships, support and safety planning. Begin with the child’s account and daily experience; distinguish observed evidence from adult interpretation.

Local application

These shared models are educational examples, not a list of officially approved Ontario programmes. Check training, licensing, local adoption and cultural suitability. A model never replaces the applicable reporting duty or legal threshold.

Model finder

Signs of Safety

Child protection mapping, danger statements, safety goals, family network work, scaling, and safety planning.

Signs of Wellbeing, Success, and Belonging

Family support, children in care, permanency, reunification, stability, identity, and belonging work.

Family Safeguarding

Whole-family child protection where parental domestic abuse, mental health, substance misuse, trauma, or adult needs affect child safety.

Contextual Safeguarding

Extra-familial harm, exploitation, peer group risk, school, neighbourhood, online spaces, and adolescent safeguarding.

Family Group Conferencing

Family-led planning, kinship care, safety planning, pre-proceedings, reunification, and support network building.

Systemic Practice

Family patterns, repeated cycles, communication, roles, meaning, conflict, and relationship-based formulation.

Motivational Interviewing

Ambivalence, parental engagement, adolescent risk, substance use, resistance, and behaviour change conversations.

Solution-Focused Practice

Goal-setting, exceptions, scaling, strengths, future-focused planning, and building on small signs of progress.

PACE and DDP-Informed Practice

Children affected by trauma, attachment disruption, care experience, shame, mistrust, and blocked trust.

Safe and Together

Domestic abuse-informed child welfare, perpetrator pattern mapping, protective parent strengths, and child impact.

Graded Care Profile 2

Neglect assessment, evidence-based care mapping, physical care, safety, love and esteem, and developmental care.

Restorative Practice

Repair, accountability, family meetings, school relationships, conflict, shame, and shared problem-solving.
