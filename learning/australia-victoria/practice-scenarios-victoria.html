Scenario Workouts — Victoria practice guide

All cases are fictional learning exercises. Explain your evidence and reasoning before reading the discussion.

Professional Foundations — Victoria

A practitioner is asked to sign a statutory assessment because they hold an AASW credential, without checking the specific appointment requirements.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Read the law and service procedure governing that assessment. Confirm qualifications, appointment and scope before signing. Bring any gap to the responsible manager and arrange an authorised practitioner while continuing work that is within your role.

Social Care and Support — Victoria

A person is told that their NDIS plan should pay for every need after a hospital admission, including treatment and unsuitable housing.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Map the actual support sought and the responsibility of each system. Obtain the relevant decisions and coordinate health, housing and disability support. Record unmet needs and interim arrangements rather than presenting an unfunded referral as a completed plan.

Decision-Making and Capacity — Victoria

A person’s advance directive refuses a particular treatment, but a relative asks the team to ignore it because they believe treatment would help.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Check validity, applicability and whether the statement is an instructional or values directive. Involve the responsible clinician and obtain advice where the position is disputed. Do not substitute the relative’s preference for the legal effect of the document or the person’s own decision.

Restrictions and Liberty — Victoria

A provider proposes a restraint copied from an old plan and assumes approval can be renewed without checking current directions.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Check whether the practice is now prohibited and whether the previous approval is relevant to the present circumstances. Assess alternatives and consult the responsible practitioner. Do not apply an obsolete approval to a new plan or bypass the Senior Practitioner’s requirements.

Mental Health — Victoria

A person is considered for a treatment order, but the proposed immediate treatment has not been arranged and a community alternative has not been explored.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Check all criteria, including that treatment will be provided and whether a less restrictive means is reasonably available. Obtain the person’s preferences and supports. The existence of illness and concern about deterioration alone does not explain the whole order decision.

Adult Safeguarding — Victoria

An adult with disability reports financial control by a partner and an unrelated failure by a support provider to deliver personal care.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Assess the family-violence concern and provider failure separately while coordinating safety. Consider the specialist family-violence route and the appropriate provider or NDIS response. Arrange continuity of care and safe contact rather than assuming one agency will handle both matters.

Children, Families and Transitions — Victoria

A school counsellor receives a disclosure of physical harm, while a colleague says the family should first be referred for voluntary parenting support.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Check the reasonable-belief and protection conditions and make the required report if met. Assess immediate safety and follow any separate police duty. Parenting support can run alongside the protection response; it does not replace it.

Rights, Culture and Recording — Victoria

A public service restricts all family visits for administrative convenience without considering a resident’s cultural and family relationships.

  • Which local framework and decision-maker apply?
  • What evidence and support could change the next step?
  • How will the person participate, challenge and obtain review?

Discussion

Identify the rights affected, the purpose of the restriction and realistic alternatives. Hear the resident and supporters they choose, consider the applicable legal duties and record the reasoning. Review the blanket policy’s effect on the individual rather than relying only on its general wording.
