Professional Foundations — Victoria

Which local framework anchors this topic in Victoria practice guide?

The Australian Association of Social Workers (AASW) Code of Ethics and Practice Standards provide a professional framework. AASW membership or accreditation, an employer requirement and statutory authority are different matters. Check the requirements for the actual role and state rather than presenting the professional association as the source of every legal power.

Which distinction must be kept clear?

Practice should attend to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples’ self-determination, cultural safety and the effects of colonisation. Ask which community-controlled organisations or cultural supports the person wants involved. Professional values need to be connected to the state law governing the particular intervention.

What are the key local actions?

Identify the employer, state, service setting and any appointment required for the task. Explain the professional role, consent and confidentiality limits before assessment. Consider power, racism, disability and material barriers alongside individual and family strengths. Use supervision and learning to test the effect of practice on the person, with the current AASW standards as a professional reference.

How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Distinguish an employer complaint, a professional-association concern and a statutory regulator or tribunal route. The relevant health, disability, aged-care or child-protection body depends on the issue. A professional membership complaint is not an appeal against a state or Commonwealth funding decision.

What common error should you avoid?

Equating association membership, clinical experience or a job title with every statutory appointment.

Social Care and Support — Victoria

Which local framework anchors this topic in Victoria practice guide?

Disability support through the NDIS, state health and housing services, and Commonwealth aged care have separate entry and funding rules. NDIS access involves age, residence and disability or early-intervention requirements; a diagnosis alone does not establish entitlement to a particular funded support. Check current NDIA rules for the actual application or plan.

Which distinction must be kept clear?

The Aged Care Act 2024 and Support at Home commenced on 1 November 2025. Do not describe the old Home Care Package arrangements as the only current system. The Commonwealth Home Support Programme has its own transition arrangements. Contributions and programme rules need a current check, including announced changes with later commencement dates.

What are the key local actions?

Identify the scheme responsible for each need and the evidence required for its assessment. Keep access eligibility, plan funding, provider availability and any client contribution separate. Consider state health, housing and community support where a need is outside a particular funded scheme. Record the person’s goals, family willingness, actual start dates and the review route for each decision.

How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

For an NDIA decision, identify whether an internal review and subsequent Administrative Review Tribunal route are available and check the decision notice’s time limits. A provider complaint to the NDIS Commission is different from a funding review. Aged-care complaints and assessment reviews use their own routes.

What common error should you avoid?

Treating NDIS eligibility as approval for every requested service, or using obsolete aged-care programme rules.

Decision-Making and Capacity — Victoria

Which local framework anchors this topic in Victoria practice guide?

The Medical Treatment Planning and Decisions Act 2016 governs important medical-consent and advance-care-directive questions. The Guardianship and Administration Act 2019 provides a separate framework for guardianship, administration and supported appointments. Specify whether the decision concerns treatment, personal matters or finances. Medical decision-making capacity concerns understanding the relevant information and effects, retaining it for the decision, considering it when choosing, and conveying the choice and related views or needs by any workable means. Provide practical support first; an apparently unwise choice does not establish incapacity.

Which distinction must be kept clear?

An advance care directive can include an instructional directive and a values directive, with different effects. Where the person cannot make the treatment decision, identify the lawful medical treatment decision maker. Their task concerns the decision the person would have made, informed by relevant preferences and values, rather than simply the representative’s preferred outcome.

What are the key local actions?

Support the person to make the specific decision and assess the relevant decision-making ability. Check for a valid, applicable instructional directive and distinguish it from a values statement. Identify the medical treatment decision maker under the applicable appointment or hierarchy. For a separate personal or financial issue, verify the scope of the relevant guardian, administrator or other arrangement.

How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

The Office of the Public Advocate (OPA) explains healthcare decision-making and routes where no appropriate decision maker is available. VCAT has relevant guardianship and other statutory functions. Disputed authority or treatment requires the route applicable to that decision, not a general family vote.

What common error should you avoid?

Treating an instructional directive as merely optional, or assuming financial administration includes medical consent.

Restrictions and Liberty — Victoria

Which local framework anchors this topic in Victoria practice guide?

The Disability Act 2006 and Victorian Senior Practitioner arrangements operate alongside the NDIS restrictive-practices framework where applicable. Authorised Program Officers, the Senior Practitioner, behaviour support practitioners and implementing providers have different responsibilities. A plan is not proof that every necessary authorisation or approval exists.

Which distinction must be kept clear?

The Senior Practitioner publishes directions and prohibitions, including a permanent-prohibition notice dated September 2025. Check the current rules for the particular practice and cohort before considering an application. A prohibited practice is not made permissible by including it in a behaviour support plan.

What are the key local actions?

Identify the actual restriction, setting and whether the disability, mental-health or aged-care framework applies. For the disability route, check the Authorised Program Officer and Senior Practitioner requirements and current prohibitions. Check NDIS plan, authorisation and incident-reporting requirements separately where they apply. Record participation, alternatives, conditions, review and the strategy for reducing the restriction.

How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

OPA, advocacy services and the relevant regulator can assist with concerns within their remits. The person needs a usable way to object and seek the applicable legal or administrative review. A guardian’s agreement does not remove the statutory restrictions on restraint.

What common error should you avoid?

Treating historical approval or a lodged plan as permanent permission, including for a prohibited practice.

Mental Health — Victoria

Which local framework anchors this topic in Victoria practice guide?

The Mental Health and Wellbeing Act 2022 replaced the 2014 Act. It prefers voluntary assessment and treatment and provides separate assessment orders, temporary treatment orders and Mental Health Tribunal treatment orders. Each stage has its own authorised decision-maker and requirements.

Which distinction must be kept clear?

Compulsory treatment criteria include mental illness; immediate treatment needed to prevent serious deterioration in health or serious harm; that treatment being provided if the order is made; and no reasonably available less restrictive means. Evidence that a service is actually available matters. Assessment-order criteria should not be replaced with the treatment-order test.

What are the key local actions?

Identify the stage, authorised practitioner and exact statutory criteria. Record the person’s preferences, available voluntary alternatives and why the proposed order is necessary. Check advance statements of preferences, nominated support persons and supported decision-making requirements. Separate treatment consent, any statutory treatment authority and additional safeguards for particular interventions.

How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Explain the statement of rights, non-legal advocacy, second psychiatric opinion and Mental Health Tribunal routes. Current handbook guidance describes their application. A nominated support person assists participation; their role should not be treated as a general substitute-consent appointment.

What common error should you avoid?

Using the assessment and treatment criteria interchangeably or treating a support person as the treatment decision-maker.

Adult Safeguarding — Victoria

Which local framework anchors this topic in Victoria practice guide?

Adult concerns may require family-violence services, police, OPA, disability safeguards, aged-care oversight or a provider complaint. Identify the relationship and service setting before selecting a route. The Family Violence Protection Act 2008 is relevant to violence and abuse within its defined relationships; not every adult concern is governed by that framework.

Which distinction must be kept clear?

The Orange Door provides access to family-violence and family-support services. NDIS and aged-care providers have their own incident and regulatory obligations. A referral for support, a reportable incident and an application for a protective order are different actions.

What are the key local actions?

Hear the adult safely and identify immediate danger, coercion and practical dependence. Identify whether the concern involves family violence, a disability or aged-care provider, another service or crime. Use the relevant specialist, police and regulatory routes, checking any mandatory reporting duty. Plan safe contact, practical support and follow-up without assuming that a referral authorises coercive action.

How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Explain the appropriate complaint, advocacy or court route for the issue. OPA can provide information about decision-making and rights within its remit. Keep the adult’s wishes and the legal authority for any intervention visible, including where agencies disagree.

What common error should you avoid?

Treating all adult safeguarding as one statutory process or assuming a guardianship referral resolves family violence.

Children, Families and Transitions — Victoria

Which local framework anchors this topic in Victoria practice guide?

The Children, Youth and Families Act 2005 distinguishes protection grounds and mandatory reporting. The mandatory-reporter categories depend on the role: do not assume every person called a social worker is included solely by title. A social worker may fall within a listed role, such as school counselling or out-of-home care work.

Which distinction must be kept clear?

DFFH guidance requires listed reporters who form the relevant reasonable belief in their work to report protection concerns involving physical injury or sexual abuse. Other forms of significant harm may also be reported. The child’s harm and parental protection must be considered; the statutory reporting duty is not identical to every possible wellbeing concern.

What are the key local actions?

Check the actual professional or employment category and the applicable reporting duty. Report to Child Protection as soon as practicable where the duty is engaged, including new grounds arising later. Consider a support referral to The Orange Door where appropriate, without substituting it for a required protection report. Check separate police and organisational duties, and plan child participation, cultural support and continuity.

How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Explain the Child Protection process and the child’s and family’s access to support or legal advice. DFFH guidance distinguishes reporting routes from family-support referrals. Advice within an organisation should not delay an applicable personal reporting obligation.

What common error should you avoid?

Assuming all social-work job titles create the same reporting duty, or confusing voluntary support with a mandatory report.

Rights, Culture and Recording — Victoria

Which local framework anchors this topic in Victoria practice guide?

The Charter of Human Rights and Responsibilities Act 2006 requires covered public authorities to act compatibly with Charter rights and give proper consideration to relevant rights, subject to statutory exceptions. Identify whether the provider is a public authority for the function involved. A Charter statement should explain the affected rights and reasoning, not simply declare compliance.

Which distinction must be kept clear?

The Equal Opportunity Act 2010, Health Records Act 2001 and Privacy and Data Protection Act 2014 have different scopes. Commonwealth law may also apply. Determine the organisation and information involved before selecting a privacy or discrimination pathway.

What are the key local actions?

Identify the relevant Charter rights and whether the decision-maker is a covered public authority. Consider the actual impact, alternatives and any lawful basis for limiting a right. Arrange accessible and culturally safe participation and address applicable equality obligations. Check the relevant privacy regime and explain access, correction, complaints and review.

How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

The Victorian Equal Opportunity and Human Rights Commission explains Charter duties and discrimination. Privacy and health complaints use their respective bodies and statutory routes. Obtain advice about the remedy for an alleged Charter breach rather than promising that every concern has a standalone Charter claim.

What common error should you avoid?

Assuming every private provider has identical Charter duties or that naming rights establishes proper consideration.
