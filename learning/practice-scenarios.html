Practice Scenarios

Use these for supervision, ASYE learning, team workshops, or private study.

Scenario 1: Care Act and Carer Stress

Mr K is 82 and lives with his daughter. He has Parkinson's disease and falls twice a week. His daughter says she is "fine" but has stopped working, sleeps on the sofa to listen for him at night, and has back pain. Mr K says he wants to stay home.

Questions:

  • What Care Act duties are engaged?
  • What would you assess?
  • Does the daughter need a carer's assessment?
  • What preventive support might reduce crisis?
  • What should be recorded?

Learning points:

  • Appearance of need is enough to assess.
  • Carer willingness and sustainability must be explored.
  • Staying home is an outcome, not a complete plan.
  • Falls, night support, equipment, medication, therapy, respite, and contingency all need analysis.

Scenario 2: Capacity and Unwise Decision

Ms A has diabetes and repeatedly eats high-sugar food. Staff say she "lacks capacity because she won't follow advice". She explains the risks of high blood sugar but says food is her only comfort since her husband died.

Questions:

  • What is the specific decision?
  • Is this incapacity or an unwise decision?
  • What support or alternatives should be considered?
  • Is there a mental health, grief, or self-neglect issue?

Learning points:

  • Risk-taking is not proof of incapacity.
  • The assessment is about understanding, retaining, using/weighing, and communicating.
  • Emotional context matters.
  • A Care Act/reablement/dietetic/mental health response may be more appropriate than a best interests decision.

Scenario 3: DoLS After the 2026 Judgment

Mr J lives in a care home. Doors are keypad locked, staff supervise him closely because he falls, and he is given sedating medication at night. He says he likes the home but becomes distressed when staff stop him leaving with his coat. He has dementia and fluctuates.

Questions:

  • What restrictions exist?
  • What are his wishes and feelings?
  • Is he objecting?
  • Can compliance be treated as consent?
  • Should a DoLS referral be made?

Learning points:

  • Use a multifactorial assessment, not the old acid test alone.
  • Distress at being stopped from leaving is important.
  • Medication and fluctuating presentation may affect expressed wishes.
  • If in doubt, refer for DoLS or seek advice.

Scenario 4: MHA, MCA, or DoLS?

Ms R is in A&E after being found in traffic saying voices told her to die. She wants to leave. She appears psychotic, frightened, and unable to discuss risk. The ward asks whether DoLS can be used because she lacks capacity.

Questions:

  • What is the purpose of detention?
  • Is treatment/assessment for mental disorder required?
  • Is MHA assessment indicated?
  • What immediate safety steps are needed?
  • What role does MCA still play?

Learning points:

  • MCA/DoLS should not be used to avoid MHA where detention for mental health assessment/treatment is required.
  • MHA assessment should consider risk, mental disorder, alternatives, and least restriction.
  • MCA may still apply to physical health or other decisions.

Scenario 5: Adult Safeguarding and Coercion

Mrs P has care and support needs and lives with her son. She says she does not want help. The son answers all questions, controls her bank card, and says professionals upset her. A neighbour reports shouting and that Mrs P has asked for food.

Questions:

  • Are Section 42 criteria reasonably suspected?
  • Can you rely on Mrs P's apparent refusal?
  • What immediate safety steps are needed?
  • How might you speak to her alone?
  • What information sharing is justified?

Learning points:

  • Coercion can undermine apparent consent.
  • Financial abuse, domestic abuse, neglect, and emotional abuse may all be relevant.
  • The adult's desired outcomes matter, but so does safe enquiry.

Scenario 6: Transition

T is 17, autistic, has an EHCP, and receives children's short breaks. Parents say T will need supported living eventually. T says they want college, gaming friends, and no "strangers deciding things". No adult services referral has been made.

Questions:

  • Is a Care Act transition assessment needed?
  • What SEND and EHCP links matter?
  • What capacity issues may arise at 16 and 18?
  • How should T's voice be supported?
  • What should happen before the 18th birthday?

Learning points:

  • Transition should start early enough to be useful.
  • Preparing for adulthood includes housing, occupation, community, relationships, and health.
  • MCA applies from 16.
  • Parents remain important, but the young person's own wishes must be central.

Scenario 7: Organisational Abuse

Several residents in a care home have unexplained bruising and pressure damage. Staff say residents are "hard to manage". Families report calls unanswered. The home uses lap belts and bedrails routinely.

Questions:

  • Individual or organisational safeguarding, or both?
  • What immediate protective actions are needed?
  • Who should be involved?
  • What records should be requested?
  • Are DoLS/MCA issues present?

Learning points:

  • Patterns matter.
  • Blanket restrictions require legal and ethical scrutiny.
  • Safeguarding, commissioning, CQC, provider action, police, MCA, and DoLS may all be relevant.
