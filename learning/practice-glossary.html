Practice Glossary

This glossary gives plain-English explanations of common social work, legal, safeguarding, mental capacity, mental health, equality, and recording terms.

Use it for revision, supervision preparation, placement learning, ASYE evidence, and quick confidence checks.

It is not a substitute for the full legislation, statutory guidance, Codes of Practice, local policy, supervision, or legal advice.

Practice glossary

Adult at risk

A shorthand phrase often used for an adult who may have care and support needs, may be experiencing or at risk of abuse or neglect, and may be unable to protect themselves because of those needs.

Advocacy

Support to help a person be involved in decisions, express views, understand information, and challenge decisions where needed.

AMHP

Approved Mental Health Professional. A trained professional with specific responsibilities under the Mental Health Act, including independent social perspective and least restrictive analysis.

Authorisation

A lawful approval for arrangements that would otherwise interfere with rights, such as DoLS authorisation or Court of Protection authorisation.

Best interests

The decision-making process used under the MCA when a person lacks capacity for a specific decision. It must consider wishes, feelings, beliefs, values, consultation, and least restriction.

Care and support needs

Needs that may arise from physical, mental, sensory, cognitive, or other impairment or illness and may affect daily living outcomes.

Carer's assessment

A Care Act assessment for a carer who may have support needs, including willingness and ability to continue caring and impact on wellbeing.

Contextual Safeguarding

A safeguarding approach that looks at extra-familial harm in peer groups, schools, neighbourhoods, transport, online spaces, and other contexts beyond the home.

Coercion

Pressure, control, intimidation, or manipulation that can undermine apparent consent, choice, or free expression.

Decision-specific capacity

Capacity is assessed for the specific decision at the specific time, not as a general status.

Defensible recording

Recording that shows facts, evidence, analysis, law or policy considered, options, rationale, dissent, and review.

Deprivation of liberty

A serious restriction of liberty requiring careful legal analysis, least restrictive thinking, and the correct authorisation route where Article 5 is engaged.

DoLS

Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards. A statutory process for authorising deprivation of liberty in hospitals and care homes where the legal criteria are met.

Eligible needs

Needs that meet the Care Act eligibility framework after considering impairment or illness, inability to achieve specified outcomes, and significant impact on wellbeing.

Family Group Conference

A family-led planning process where the wider family network is supported to make a safe plan for a child, subject to professional safety checks and review.

Family Safeguarding

A whole-family model that connects child protection with adult-facing work around domestic abuse, mental health, substance misuse, and parenting capacity.

GCP2

Graded Care Profile 2. A structured tool used in some areas to assess the quality of care and support analysis of neglect.

Human rights analysis

A structured check of rights such as liberty, family life, dignity, life, non-discrimination, and fair process.

IMCA

Independent Mental Capacity Advocate. A safeguard for certain serious decisions where a person lacks capacity and has no appropriate person to consult.

Least restrictive option

The option that meets the legitimate aim while interfering least with the person's rights, freedoms, relationships, and autonomy.

Making Safeguarding Personal

A person-led approach to safeguarding that focuses on the adult's views, desired outcomes, rights, safety, and control.

Mental disorder

The Mental Health Act uses this broad concept when considering assessment or treatment under the Act. Practitioners must check current law, Code of Practice, and local policy.

Motivational Interviewing

A collaborative approach to conversations about change, useful where a parent or young person feels ambivalent, stuck, defensive, or unsure.

Ordinary residence

A Care Act concept used to decide which local authority is responsible for meeting eligible needs in some situations.

PACE

Playfulness, Acceptance, Curiosity, and Empathy. A relationship-based stance often used in trauma and attachment-informed work with children.

Person-centred

Practice that keeps the person's voice, outcomes, rights, culture, relationships, strengths, and lived experience central.

Professional curiosity

The habit of respectfully looking beyond first accounts, testing assumptions, noticing patterns, and seeking missing information.

Proportionality

A rights-based test of whether an intervention is suitable, necessary, and balanced against the impact on the person.

Reasonable adjustments

Changes that reduce disadvantage for disabled people and support fair access, communication, assessment, and involvement.

Safe and Together

A domestic abuse-informed child welfare model that maps perpetrator patterns, protective parent strengths, and the impact on children.

Section 42 enquiry

A Care Act safeguarding duty to make or cause enquiries where the statutory criteria are reasonably suspected.

Section 117 aftercare

A joint aftercare duty for people detained under qualifying Mental Health Act sections, linked to needs arising from mental disorder.

Self-neglect

A complex safeguarding and wellbeing concern involving serious neglect of personal care, health, home environment, or safety.

Signs of Safety

A child protection practice approach using clear worries, what is working well, what needs to happen, safety goals, scaling, and family network planning.

Solution-Focused Practice

A practice approach that explores goals, exceptions, scaling, strengths, and small signs of progress that can be repeated.

Substantial difficulty

A Care Act advocacy concept covering serious difficulty understanding, retaining, using or weighing information, or communicating views.

Transition assessment

Assessment for young people or carers approaching adulthood where likely adult care and support needs may arise.

Wellbeing

A broad Care Act concept covering dignity, physical and mental health, protection from abuse, control, relationships, accommodation, participation, and contribution to society.
