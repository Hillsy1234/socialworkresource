Revision cards

Professional Foundations: What should professional curiosity help a social worker do?

Look beyond the first account, test assumptions, notice patterns, and ask respectful questions about risk, strengths, culture, relationships, and lived experience.

Professional Foundations: What is the purpose of supervision in complex practice?

To create space for reflection, legal reasoning, emotional impact, risk analysis, accountability, and clear next steps.

Professional Foundations: What does strengths-based practice avoid?

It avoids treating optimism as evidence. It recognises agency, relationships, culture, and resources while still analysing needs and risks.

Professional Foundations: What should good recording show?

The person's voice, relevant evidence, analysis, options considered, legal basis, rationale, dissent, and review plan.

Professional Foundations: Why is anti-oppressive practice not a separate task?

Because power, discrimination, access, culture, communication, trauma, and rights shape every assessment, plan, and professional decision.

Care Act 2014: What is the central organising principle of the Care Act?

The person's wellbeing, including dignity, control, protection from abuse, relationships, participation, accommodation, and contribution to society.

Care Act 2014: When should a Care Act assessment be offered?

When it appears an adult may have needs for care and support, regardless of whether those needs are likely to be eligible.

Care Act 2014: What is the eligibility route under the Care Act?

Needs arise from or relate to impairment or illness, the adult cannot achieve specified outcomes, and this has a significant impact on wellbeing.

Care Act 2014: When is an independent advocate required?

When the person would have substantial difficulty being involved and there is no appropriate person to support their involvement.

Care Act 2014: What do TDB and Re Direct Payments add to care planning?

Investigate decision-specific capacity doubts relevant to needs (TDB v Haringey [2026] EWCA Civ 1184); participation support does not replace that assessment. Verify specific deputy authority for managing direct payments (Re Direct Payments [2026] EWCOP 42 (T2)); a standard financial order is insufficient. The latter is a Tier 2 illustration, not binding appellate authority.

Mental Capacity Act 2005: What is the starting presumption under the MCA?

A person is presumed to have capacity unless it is established that they lack capacity for the specific decision at the specific time. TDB v Haringey confirms that the presumption is not a reason to avoid assessment where relevant capacity is in doubt; it does not require formal assessment in every case.

Mental Capacity Act 2005: What must be done before concluding a person lacks capacity?

All practicable steps must be taken to support the person to make the decision, unless those steps are unsuccessful.

Mental Capacity Act 2005: What are the four functional abilities in a capacity assessment?

Understand relevant information, retain it long enough, use or weigh it, and communicate the decision by any means.

Mental Capacity Act 2005: What does best interests decision-making require?

Consider the person's wishes, feelings, beliefs, values, least restrictive options, relevant circumstances, consultation, and whether capacity may return.

Mental Capacity Act 2005: When might an IMCA be needed?

For certain serious decisions when the person lacks capacity and has no appropriate family or friend to consult, subject to the statutory criteria.

DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty: Where can DoLS authorisation apply?

In hospitals and care homes where there is reason to believe Article 5 may be engaged and the person lacks relevant capacity.

DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty: What is the least restrictive principle asking practitioners to do?

Choose the option that interferes least with the person's rights and freedom while still meeting the necessary aim.

DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty: Why must deprivation of liberty analysis be carefully evidenced?

Because Article 5 protects liberty. Restrictions, consent, capacity, objection, purpose, duration, supervision, and practical reality all matter.

DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty: When might the Court of Protection be needed?

For community deprivation of liberty, serious disputes, complex best interests decisions, or situations outside ordinary DoLS authorisation.

DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty: What do the October 2026 liberty cases illustrate?

Re BM [2026] EWCOP 48 (T2) distinguishes contentment with life from a meaningful view of restrictions. Re PK & MK [2026] EWCOP 52 (T2) considers continuing safeguards when Article 5 is not engaged. Both are fact-specific Tier 2 illustrations, not universal rules.

Mental Health Act: What is the broad purpose of the Mental Health Act?

To provide a legal framework for compulsory assessment or treatment of mental disorder when the statutory criteria are met.

Mental Health Act: What does an AMHP bring to MHA decision-making?

Independent social perspective, least restrictive analysis, rights focus, family and community context, risk analysis, and scrutiny of alternatives.

Mental Health Act: How should the MCA/MHA interface be checked?

Ordinary MCA powers do not authorise deprivation of liberty. Check separate DoLS or court authority, section 4A, Schedule 1A eligibility and section 28 treatment limits. Non-objection alone does not establish eligibility, and DoLS does not authorise treatment.

Mental Health Act: What is Section 117 aftercare?

A joint duty to provide aftercare for eligible people detained under qualifying MHA sections, linked to needs arising from mental disorder.

Mental Health Act: Which MHA 2025 commencement dates must not be missed?

Section 56(1) provisions commenced on 18 February 2026, including supervised-discharge changes for relevant restricted patients. Sections 51 and 52 followed on 6 April. Check each provision; these dates do not make every reform operative.

Safeguarding Adults: What are the three Section 42 enquiry trigger questions?

Does the adult have care and support needs, are they experiencing or at risk of abuse or neglect, and are they unable to protect themselves because of those needs?

Safeguarding Adults: What does Making Safeguarding Personal require?

Work with the person to understand desired outcomes, rights, risk, consent, safety, control, and what protection means to them.

Safeguarding Adults: Why is consent important in adult safeguarding?

Because adults with capacity usually make their own decisions, but information-sharing or action may still be justified in specific risk or public interest situations.

Safeguarding Adults: What should a safeguarding plan include?

Desired outcomes, protective actions, roles, risk controls, contingency, review arrangements, communication needs, and escalation routes.

Safeguarding Adults: Why can self-neglect be complex?

It may involve autonomy, capacity, trauma, mental disorder, executive functioning, environmental risk, coercion, and serious harm.

Children, Families, and Transitions: What does whole-family practice mean?

Understanding the adult, child, carer, family network, risks, strengths, relationships, and how each person's needs affect the others.

Children, Families, and Transitions: Why are transitions important in social work?

Young people may move between children's and adults' systems, so planning should start early and keep outcomes, rights, education, health, care, and family roles connected.

Children, Families, and Transitions: What should practitioners consider for young carers?

The caring role, impact on wellbeing and education, hidden responsibilities, family support, assessment rights, and appropriate services.

Children, Families, and Transitions: What is a key risk in transition planning?

The young person falling between services because eligibility, responsibility, timing, or information-sharing is unclear.

Children, Families, and Transitions: Which September 2026 safeguarding changes are now operative in England?

KCSIE 2026 replaced the previous edition on 1 September. The section 16LA children’s information-sharing duty took effect on 30 September: use the final DfE guidance and check its scope, conditions and data-protection safeguards.

Equality, Human Rights, and Recording: What is the purpose of reasonable adjustments?

To remove or reduce disadvantage for disabled people so they can access assessment, communication, services, decisions, and participation fairly.

Equality, Human Rights, and Recording: Do safeguards end when Article 5 is not engaged?

No. Article 8, MCA and Care Act duties can still require participation, lawful best-interests decisions, proportionate restrictions, advocacy where eligible, review and safeguarding. Re PK & MK is a fact-specific illustration, not a universal requirement for ongoing court supervision.

Equality, Human Rights, and Recording: What does proportionate recording mean?

Recording enough evidence, analysis, rationale, and review detail to justify the decision without unnecessary or irrelevant information.

Equality, Human Rights, and Recording: What should be recorded when views differ?

The different views, evidence, professional analysis, how disagreement was considered, and the reason for the final decision.

Equality, Human Rights, and Recording: How does equality analysis strengthen practice?

It helps identify barriers, discrimination, communication needs, cultural context, power imbalance, and steps needed for fair involvement.
