Professional Foundations — New York

Learning outcomes

Explain your professional role, distinguish an ethical concern from a statutory power, and turn a formulation into an accountable plan.

Local legislation, policy and professional framework

New York Education Law and the Office of the Professions distinguish Licensed Master Social Workers (LMSWs) from Licensed Clinical Social Workers (LCSWs). An LMSW may provide clinical social-work services, including diagnosis, psychotherapy and assessment-based treatment planning, only under the required supervision; an LCSW may provide those services independently within competence.

Licensure, registration, a permitted practice setting and any separate mental-hygiene designation must be checked. Employer seniority does not create a statutory power. Professional ethical guidance should be read alongside state law and applicable agency obligations.

From information to a professional formulation

A chronology says what happened. A formulation explains how the available evidence, the person's experience and their environment might connect. Start with the person's desired change. Map relationships, housing, income, health, discrimination and service barriers alongside individual strengths. A missed appointment may reflect fear, inaccessible transport or an unsafe household; it does not establish unwillingness to engage.

Use at least two possible explanations for a difficult situation. State what evidence supports each and what would challenge it. A strengths approach should identify actual resources the person can use, with their agreement. It should not turn an absent service into an assumed family responsibility. A trauma-informed conversation offers predictability, choice and control over pace while keeping necessary enquiries clear.

Boundaries and supervision

Explain who employs you, the purpose of contact, how information will be used and the limits of confidentiality. Separate an invitation to accept support from an action the law permits without agreement. Ask for supervision when an instruction conflicts with professional duties or exceeds your authority. Record the issue, advice, action and unresolved concern; merely recording that a manager agreed does not explain the decision.

For a learning reflection, choose one decision you influenced. Describe what the person said, your initial assumption, what changed your understanding and what you will do differently. Seek feedback about the effect on the person, rather than counting training attendance as evidence of improved practice.

Apply the local pathway

  • Confirm the licence, current registration, scope and supervision required for the actual work.
  • Explain the professional role, service purpose and confidentiality limits.
  • Check any additional designation or qualification required for a statutory mental-health function.
  • Use supervision and continuing education to improve the effect of practice, including cultural responsiveness and access.

Rights, challenge and review

The Office of the Professions addresses professional regulation; benefit decisions, agency services and mental-health detention have different complaint or review routes. Explain which body can address the person’s concern and desired remedy.

Worked learning example

Fictional case: An LMSW is offered an independent psychotherapy caseload without the supervision required for clinical work.

Before reading the discussion, identify the relevant decision, the missing evidence and the person who can lawfully act.

Discussion and reasoning

Check the scope, setting and supervision requirements before accepting the arrangement. Arrange appropriate oversight or a lawful alternative and explain the practitioner’s status accurately. A social-work licence does not mean that both licence categories have identical clinical authority.

Common error to avoid

Treating LMSW and LCSW practice permissions as interchangeable.

Recording and supervision exercise

Use the matching printable prompt to make a short record for this case. Include:

  • Your role and the legal or professional framework
  • The person’s outcomes and communication preferences
  • Evidence, competing explanations and missing perspectives
  • Power, boundaries and any conflict of interest
  • Agreed action, supervision, feedback and review

Ask a supervisor to identify a missing fact that could change your conclusion. Revise the plan to show who will obtain that fact and what happens while it remains uncertain.
