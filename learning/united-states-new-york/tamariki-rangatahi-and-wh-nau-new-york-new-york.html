Children, Families and Transitions — New York

Learning outcomes

Separate family support from protection powers, apply the local reporting duty, involve the child and coordinate education, health and transition planning.

Local legislation, policy and professional framework

Social Services Law sections 413 and 415 identify mandated reporters and reporting procedures. Covered social workers must act on reasonable cause to suspect abuse or maltreatment within the statutory circumstances. The Statewide Central Register route has defined scope, including the relationship of the alleged responsible person to the child.

An immediate report and the required written follow-up are distinct steps; section 415 generally requires the written report within 48 hours. Abuse by someone outside the child-protective reporting definition may still require police or another safety response. Do not treat an out-of-scope SCR decision as proof that no action is needed.

Understand the child’s daily experience

Use age-appropriate communication, observation and accessible material. Ask about ordinary routines, trusted adults, worries, food, sleep, school and relationships. Record the child's words separately from adult interpretations. For a baby or a child who does not use speech, describe interactions and behaviour without claiming to know a view they have not expressed.

Assess harm, strengths and the practical conditions affecting care. Poverty, inaccessible services or overcrowded housing may require material help; they should not automatically be treated as evidence of parental indifference. At the same time, a family's strengths do not remove the need to act on a protection concern. Explore the experience of siblings and whether a proposed protective adult can actually provide the support being described.

Keep support and compulsory intervention clear

Explain your role, the concern, confidentiality limits and the next step in language the family can understand. Establish the local reporting threshold and receiving authority. A practice model can organise enquiry but cannot determine a statutory threshold or authorise removal. Where you disagree with a decision, identify the evidence and use the relevant escalation process without delaying necessary immediate action.

Plan with education, health, disability and community services, identifying each person's responsibility. For a young person approaching adulthood, address accommodation, income, healthcare, relationships, education or work and decision support before a service ends. Confirm eligibility and acceptance by adult services rather than assuming that a childhood plan transfers automatically. Preserve cultural, family and community relationships in ways consistent with the child's safety and wishes.

Apply the local pathway

  • Identify the reporter role, child’s age, alleged responsible person and facts supporting reasonable cause.
  • Use the required SCR reporting process and immediate emergency response where necessary.
  • Complete the applicable written follow-up and document receipt, facts and safety actions.
  • Consider police, institutional or other reporting duties separately and coordinate family support.

Rights, challenge and review

Explain the child-protective process and access to support or legal advice. Reporting is not a court determination of abuse or authority for every intervention. Preserve the child’s account and the distinction between an allegation, investigation and finding.

Worked learning example

Fictional case: A social worker hears a disclosure but is told to wait for a manager to decide whether it is sufficiently certain to report.

Before reading the discussion, identify the relevant decision, the missing evidence and the person who can lawfully act.

Discussion and reasoning

Assess reasonable cause under the statute and act through the required procedure. Record the relationship and facts that determine the correct recipient. Do not require certainty or delay a required report for an internal meeting.

Common error to avoid

Confusing reasonable cause with proof, or assuming the SCR is the only possible route for every threat to a child.

Recording and supervision exercise

Use the matching printable prompt to make a short record for this case. Include:

  • Child’s account, communication and daily-life evidence
  • Harm, strengths, needs and alternative explanations
  • Reporting duty, recipient and action taken
  • Family participation, culture and legal authority
  • Named plan, education/health links and transition dates

Ask a supervisor to identify a missing fact that could change your conclusion. Revise the plan to show who will obtain that fact and what happens while it remains uncertain.
