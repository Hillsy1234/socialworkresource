# Theory Into Practice

## Purpose

Social work theory helps practitioners move from description to analysis. It gives structure to professional curiosity: what might be happening, why might it be happening, what evidence supports that idea, and what else could explain the same facts?

Theory should never be used to label a person or force a case into a favourite framework. Use it to ask better questions, test assumptions, and make recording more transparent.

## The Hypothesis Rule

A practice hypothesis is a careful, testable idea. It is not a conclusion.

Use this structure:

```text
I wonder whether...
This might be because...
Evidence that supports this is...
Evidence that challenges this is...
The person's own view is...
An alternative explanation is...
I will test this by...
I will review this on...
```

## Theory and Hypothesis Map

### Ecological Systems Theory

This lens looks at the person within their environment: family, housing, income, community, services, culture, policy, and social conditions.

Possible hypothesis:

- The issue may be shaped by environmental pressure, poverty, service gaps, inaccessible systems, isolation, or housing conditions rather than only individual behaviour.

Questions to test:

- What changed around the person before risk escalated?
- Which systems are helping, and which are adding pressure?
- Would risk reduce if practical circumstances changed?

### Strengths-Based Practice

This lens looks for capabilities, relationships, community, identity, culture, coping, and what is already working.

Possible hypothesis:

- The person may have protective factors that are being hidden by a risk-heavy professional account.

Questions to test:

- What has helped the person cope so far?
- Who does the person trust?
- Which strengths are evidenced, and which are hopeful assumptions?

### Trauma-Informed Practice

This lens asks how trauma, coercion, loss, shame, threat, and lack of control may affect behaviour and engagement.

Possible hypothesis:

- Behaviour that appears resistant, angry, avoidant, or disengaged may be a survival response to fear, trauma, or previous harm.

Questions to test:

- What might feel unsafe or controlling to the person?
- How can choice and predictability be increased?
- Are professional actions repeating past experiences of powerlessness?

### Attachment Theory

This lens explores how early and later relationship experiences may shape trust, dependency, separation, rejection, and care-seeking.

Possible hypothesis:

- The person's response to support may reflect previous experiences of unreliable, unsafe, or lost relationships.

Questions to test:

- What happens when support increases, reduces, or changes?
- Are there patterns of mistrust, clinging, withdrawal, or testing?
- What professional relationship style would be consistent and boundaried?

### Systems and Family Theory

This lens looks at roles, rules, patterns, communication, conflict, caregiving dynamics, and how one person's change affects the wider network.

Possible hypothesis:

- The presenting issue may be maintained by family roles, conflict patterns, service responses, or unspoken expectations.

Questions to test:

- Who is expected to do what in the family or network?
- What changes when professionals become involved?
- Who benefits, loses, or feels threatened if the pattern changes?

### Anti-Oppressive and Anti-Discriminatory Practice

This lens examines power, inequality, discrimination, racism, disability, class, gender, sexuality, age, culture, stigma, and access.

Possible hypothesis:

- The concern may be intensified by structural inequality, professional power, discrimination, inaccessible services, or cultural misunderstanding.

Questions to test:

- Whose account is being believed most easily?
- What barriers has the person faced before this assessment?
- What advocacy, reasonable adjustments, or cultural humility are needed?

### Crisis Theory

This lens focuses on immediate stress, temporary disequilibrium, reduced coping, narrowed options, and the need for stabilisation.

Possible hypothesis:

- The person or family may be temporarily overwhelmed and need safety, containment, practical support, and short-term planning before deeper work is possible.

Questions to test:

- What event or loss has overwhelmed usual coping?
- What must happen in the next 24 to 72 hours?
- What can wait until the person is safer and more stable?

### Life Course Theory

This lens considers transitions, cumulative disadvantage, ageing, bereavement, identity, timing, and turning points.

Possible hypothesis:

- Current risk may connect to cumulative exclusion, earlier life experiences, loss, identity change, or transition.

Questions to test:

- What transition is happening now?
- What earlier events still shape trust, identity, or options?
- What strengths from the person's life story can be brought into planning?

### Task-Centred Practice

This lens breaks complex problems into agreed, specific, achievable tasks with clear roles and review.

Possible hypothesis:

- The difficulty may feel unmanageable because it has not yet been broken into negotiated, practical next steps.

Questions to test:

- What problem does the person want to work on first?
- What is the smallest useful next step?
- Who will do what, by when, and how will we know it helped?

## Recording a Theory-Informed Analysis

Use this structure in supervision notes or assessment analysis:

```text
Theory lens considered:
Why this lens may fit:
Evidence supporting the hypothesis:
Evidence challenging the hypothesis:
Person's own explanation:
Alternative explanations:
Legal and rights issues:
Risk and protective factors:
Action to test the hypothesis:
Review point:
```

## Common Pitfalls

- Using theory to label the person rather than understand context.
- Treating one theory as the whole explanation.
- Ignoring culture, discrimination, poverty, trauma, or power.
- Confusing a hypothesis with evidence.
- Forgetting capacity, consent, legal duties, safeguarding, and human rights.
- Recording professional interpretation without the person's own account.

## Supervision Prompts

- What am I assuming?
- What theory lens am I using, and why?
- What evidence would challenge my view?
- What does the person say is happening?
- What social, cultural, economic, or service factors are shaping this?
- How might my power or organisational role affect the assessment?
- What would be the least restrictive, rights-respecting next step?

## Applying these lenses in Wales

Use the Welsh care and support, safeguarding and education frameworks when moving from a hypothesis to action. The finder suggests questions to explore; it does not diagnose a person or determine legal thresholds. Test more than one explanation, seek contrary evidence and agree a review point with the person.
