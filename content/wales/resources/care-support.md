# Care and Support in Wales

## Learning outcomes

Distinguish a request for assessment from eligibility and charging decisions; include the person's outcomes and the carer's own situation; explain how assessment leads to a recorded plan and review.

## The legal map

The Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014 provides the framework. Part 3 covers assessment, Part 4 meeting needs, Part 5 charging and Part 10 advocacy. Adult, child and carer assessment provisions are separate: sections 19, 21 and 24 respectively. Use the [Welsh codes collection](https://www.gov.wales/social-services-codes-practice) and the [Part 3 assessment Code](https://www.gov.wales/assessing-care-and-support-needs-individuals-code-practice) alongside the current Act and regulations. An assessment is not a promise of a particular service.

## Start with a conversation

Ask what a good day looks like, what has changed, and what the person wants to retain or regain. Explore abilities as well as difficulties. Clarify the person's preferred language, communication support and who they want involved. A relative's presence should not replace the person's own account. Offer opportunities to speak privately where appropriate.

Build a picture across daily living, relationships, housing, access to community, health and safety. Distinguish what the person can do consistently from what they managed once under ideal conditions. Describe informal help accurately: who provides it, whether they are willing, and whether it is sustainable. Avoid describing an unavailable family member as a resource that meets a need.

## Assessment, eligibility and planning

Assessment identifies needs and outcomes; eligibility applies the relevant Welsh criteria; planning explains how needs will be met. A financial assessment is a distinct process. Do not infer that financial resources remove the need for a needs assessment. Check refusal and exception provisions where a person declines an assessment.

In the analysis, connect each identified need to evidence and a desired outcome. Consider preventive and community responses alongside commissioned support, and test their availability. Explain why a proposed option is workable for this individual. Record unmet needs and disagreements rather than obscuring them in a service list. Set who will do what, when implementation will be checked, and how the person can raise concerns.

## Carers and advocacy

Give carers space to discuss their own wellbeing, employment, relationships and willingness to continue caring. Do not assume that a shared assessment means identical wishes or consent. Check the [Part 10 advocacy Code](https://www.gov.wales/advocacy-services-code-practice) where barriers affect participation; distinguish independent professional advocacy from family support and from advocacy under mental capacity or mental health legislation.

## Charging, direct payments and cross-border work

Refer to the current Welsh charging Code and direct-payment rules before discussing financial expectations. This resource deliberately does not hard-code financial limits. For a move or placement across a border, establish the arranging authority, existing plan, funding arrangement, ordinary-residence question and applicable statutory provisions. Obtain a named handover and written agreement; do not assume physical location alone transfers responsibility. See the [Part 11 Code entry in the codes collection](https://www.gov.wales/social-services-codes-practice).

## Worked learning example

Fictional example: Elin wants to remain at home after two falls. Her daughter visits daily but is exhausted. Separate Elin's mobility and daily-living needs from her daughter's support needs. Ask about language preference, environmental changes and the reliability of proposed help. Compare options against Elin's outcomes, document the daughter's actual willingness, and agree what happens if a visit is missed. “Family will support” is not a sufficient contingency.

## Recording check

- Person's account, outcomes and communication support.
- Evidence of needs, strengths, risks and carer circumstances.
- Relevant assessment and eligibility reasoning, with source checked.
- Advocacy consideration, options and disagreements.
- Named actions, interim arrangements, review triggers and contact route.
