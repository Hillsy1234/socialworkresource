## Professional Foundations


### Which local framework anchors this topic in Wales practice guide?

Social Care Wales’s new Codes of Professional Practice took effect in July 2026. Use the current worker Code and distinguish it from the employer Code, local-authority procedures and statutory appointments. The professional framework applies across different settings, but the legal power for an intervention depends on the actual function.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

A person’s preferred language and ability to participate belong in the first conversation, formulation and plan. Record how supervision, feedback and professional learning change practice rather than only listing completed training.


### What are the key local actions?

Check the current Social Care Wales Code and the requirements of the actual role. Explain the legal or voluntary purpose of involvement and confidentiality limits. Make language, accessibility, power and service barriers visible in assessment. Distinguish a professional concern, service complaint and statutory decision challenge.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Social Care Wales addresses professional regulation; the local authority, provider and relevant statutory review bodies address different service decisions. Explain the route that can provide the remedy the person seeks.


### What common error should you avoid?

Treating an old induction handout as the current professional standard.


## Care and Support in Wales


### Which local framework anchors this topic in Wales practice guide?

The Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014 separates adult, child and carer assessments in sections 19, 21 and 24. Part 4 and the eligibility regulations govern the next stage; the Part 5 charging decision is separate. Use the current Welsh assessment, meeting-needs, charging and advocacy Codes.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

Eligibility requires the applicable needs-and-outcomes analysis and consideration of whether the person can meet those needs alone, with willing others or through accessible community services, or requires the authority’s involvement. A service that exists on a directory but cannot be accessed is not an established solution.


### What are the key local actions?

Identify the correct assessment duty and explain the person’s outcomes and evidence of need. Apply the relevant Welsh eligibility regulations rather than a test imported from another system. Check participation barriers and the independent professional advocacy requirements. Record the support plan, separate charging decision, unmet needs and review or complaint route.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Ask for written reasons and the relevant local-authority review or complaints process. Consider independent advocacy where required to make participation effective. A disagreement over charges should not erase the needs assessment or interim support plan.


### What common error should you avoid?

Using a theoretically available service or an unwilling relative to conclude that a need is already met.


## Mental Capacity Act 2005


### Which local framework anchors this topic in Wales practice guide?

The Mental Capacity Act 2005 applies to the specific decision and time. The person must be unable to decide because of an impairment or disturbance of mind or brain; inability concerns understanding, retaining, using or weighing relevant information, or communicating the decision. Support must be tried and an unwise choice is not itself incapacity.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

An attorney, deputy, advance decision and a professional acting under the Act have different roles and limits. A best-interests decision should preserve participation and consider wishes, feelings, beliefs, values and less restrictive options. It does not itself answer every deprivation-of-liberty or mental-health authority question.


### What are the key local actions?

Specify the decision and record support and evidence against the relevant abilities. Explain the causal connection to impairment if concluding that capacity is lacking. Check any attorney, deputy or advance decision and the scope of its authority. Record best-interests reasoning, consultation, disagreement and the appropriate challenge route.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

The Court of Protection deals with relevant disputes and decisions. Consider statutory advocacy requirements and specialist advice where serious disagreement or liberty issues arise. A relative’s agreement should not substitute for the required legal analysis.


### What common error should you avoid?

Treating professional disagreement, a diagnosis or a risk score as the capacity test.


## DoLS and Deprivation of Liberty


### Which local framework anchors this topic in Wales practice guide?

The Supreme Court’s June 2026 judgment requires the liberty analysis to distinguish the Article 5 consent question from domestic decision-making capacity. The old two-part checklist should not be used as the sole answer. Mere acquiescence does not establish valid consent.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

Where required, the hospital or care-home DoLS route and community Court of Protection arrangements have different scope. The actual restrictions, the person’s understanding and wishes, the authorisation and the care or treatment decisions should remain separate in the record.


### What are the key local actions?

Record the actual setting, restrictions, duration and response to a wish to leave. Use current judgment and Welsh guidance with specialist advice on the applicable legal test. Check the correct authorisation route, representation, conditions and review. Explore less restrictive alternatives and act on objections or changed circumstances.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Explain the representative, advocacy and court challenge arrangements that apply. An existing authorisation should be reviewed through the responsible process; do not simply remove safeguards on the basis of a changed summary or training slide.


### What common error should you avoid?

Equating silence with consent or treating lack of capacity as the complete liberty analysis.


## Mental Health in Wales


### Which local framework anchors this topic in Wales practice guide?

Use the Mental Health Act 1983 with the Welsh Code of Practice and commenced amendments. The Mental Health (Wales) Measure 2010 adds distinct duties concerning primary mental-health support, care coordination and planning, reassessment of eligible former service users and advocacy. It is not simply another detention route.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

Part 2 care and treatment planning concerns outcomes, services, responsibilities and review. Part 3 provides a direct reassessment route for eligible former secondary-service users, subject to its conditions. New mental-health reform provisions must be checked for commencement before changing operational procedures.


### What are the key local actions?

Identify the exact assessment, detention, treatment or Measure duty involved. Use the Welsh Code and current statutory forms and commencement information. For care planning, record outcomes, named services, responsibilities and review. Explain reassessment and advocacy rights appropriate to the person’s eligibility and status.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

The Mental Health Review Tribunal for Wales and other statutory review routes address particular detention questions. Measure care planning and reassessment are separate rights. Explain which process addresses the person’s concern and how to obtain support or legal advice.


### What common error should you avoid?

Treating every mental-health right as a detention issue or overlooking Measure reassessment duties.


## Safeguarding Adults in Wales


### Which local framework anchors this topic in Wales practice guide?

Section 126 of the Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014 concerns an adult experiencing or at risk of abuse or neglect, with care and support needs, who cannot protect themselves because of those needs. The authority’s reasonable-cause-to-suspect enquiry duty and relevant partners’ reporting duties are different.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

A capacity finding is not a substitute for the adult-at-risk analysis. Coercion, dependence, practical barriers and the adult’s own desired outcomes need attention. Reporting, enquiries, service support and authority for any imposed intervention must be distinguished.


### What are the key local actions?

Record evidence against the adult-at-risk elements and address immediate safety. Identify your reporting responsibility and the authority responsible for enquiries. Use the Wales Safeguarding Procedures and the applicable statutory guidance. Record participation, lawful sharing, accepted responsibilities and review of outcomes.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Explain the local-authority and provider complaint processes and relevant advocacy or legal support. A safeguarding referral does not automatically authorise removal, treatment or management of finances. Check the law for the particular proposed action.


### What common error should you avoid?

Using capacity as the only safeguarding threshold or treating reporting and enquiries as the same duty.


## Children, Families and Transitions


### Which local framework anchors this topic in Wales practice guide?

Welsh care and support, child protection and education duties need to be mapped separately. The Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act 2014 works alongside the Children Act framework and the Additional Learning Needs and Education Tribunal (Wales) Act 2018.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

An Individual Development Plan (IDP) records additional learning needs and provision within the Welsh education framework. It does not replace a care and support plan or child-protection response. Welsh Government published further ALN decision-making guidance and example IDP forms in 2026; use these with the Code and statutory duties.


### What are the key local actions?

Identify the child’s support and protection needs and the relevant Welsh statutory route. Hear the child and family with suitable language and communication support. Check who prepares and maintains the IDP and how education, health and social care contributions connect. Plan transition with named responsibilities and confirm actual acceptance by the next service.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Disagreement about an ALN decision can involve the applicable reconsideration, disagreement-resolution and Education Tribunal for Wales routes. A social-care complaint follows a different process. Help the family identify the decision and remedy without allowing one dispute to halt other necessary support.


### What common error should you avoid?

Treating an education plan as a substitute for care assessment or a completed transition handover.


## Rights, Language and Recording


### Which local framework anchors this topic in Wales practice guide?

The Human Rights Act 1998, Equality Act 2010, applicable data-protection law and Welsh-language duties engage different parts of a decision. More than just words promotes the Active Offer of Welsh-language care, recognising language as part of safe and effective support.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

Distinguish a person’s language need and the organisation’s applicable Welsh-language standards from a general translation preference. Record how communication affects participation, consent, assessment and the reliability of evidence. A completed English form is not proof that the person could take part effectively.


### What are the key local actions?

Ask and record language and communication needs and make the Active Offer practical. Identify the actual equality, human-rights and Welsh-language duties of the service. Check the purpose and lawful basis for information sharing, with appropriate limits. Explain access, correction, complaints and the relevant rights or language challenge route.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

A Welsh-language standards concern, a privacy complaint and a challenge to the care decision may require different bodies. Explain the organisation’s procedure and relevant external route. Address immediate access barriers while a complaint is considered.


### What common error should you avoid?

Treating language access as cosmetic or using a communication barrier as evidence of incapacity.
