# Children, Families and Transitions

## Learning outcomes

Connect Welsh care/support, child protection, education and transition frameworks while keeping the child's account distinct from adult service concerns.

## Use the right framework for the question

The Social Services and Well-being (Wales) Act includes child assessment and support duties. Child-protection work also requires the relevant Children Act provisions and Welsh safeguarding guidance. Do not use England's Working Together document as the Wales procedure. Refer to [Welsh statutory guidance](https://socialcare.wales/resources-guidance/information-and-learning-hub/sswbact/statutory-guidance), including Working Together to Safeguard People Volume 5, and the [Wales Safeguarding Procedures](https://safeguarding.wales/).

Distinguish a child needing care and support, a child potentially at risk, a child looked after, and a young carer. Several frameworks can be relevant simultaneously. A service allocation or adult safeguarding referral does not remove the need to consider a child's circumstances separately.

## The child's lived experience

Describe daily life: meals, sleep, school, relationships, safety, care responsibilities and who the child turns to. Use developmentally appropriate communication and the child's preferred language. Notice discrepancies between adults' explanations and the child's account without assuming which is correct. Explain confidentiality and information sharing in a way the child can understand.

When an adult is unwell or support arrangements fail, ask who takes over tasks. A child who appears capable may still be carrying responsibilities with consequences for education, rest or relationships. Make the child's support needs visible rather than treating their help as part of an adult care package.

## ALN and Individual Development Plans

Wales uses the additional learning needs framework and Individual Development Plans. The ALN Code sets out duties for schools, further-education institutions, local authorities and NHS bodies. The legal definition, responsibility for a plan, timeframes and review/challenge routes must be checked for the learner's circumstances. An IDP is not an English EHCP with a different heading. [ALN Code and official IDP examples](https://www.gov.wales/additional-learning-needs-code).

## Transition is a coordinated process

Start by mapping what is changing and when: education, support provider, home, funding, consent/decision-making arrangements and professional contacts. Do not assume support continues unchanged on a birthday, or that it must automatically stop. Clarify the applicable assessment and planning duties, identify gaps and name who is resolving each one.

Ask what the young person wants their adult life to look like. Consider communication, relationships, transport, employment or learning, cultural identity and family support. Make separate space for carers' needs. Use a shared timeline with evidence of handover and a contingency if the next service is delayed.

## Family models and safeguarding

Models can help organise conversations and hypotheses; they do not replace legal analysis or the child's evidence. Check training, local adoption and whether a method is appropriate where there is coercion or unsafe family contact. The model finder suggests topics for supervision, not a national requirement or an automated plan.

## Worked learning example

Fictional example: Seren is 17, has learning and communication needs and is leaving school. Her parent assumes adult support and transport are already arranged. Identify current plans, named contacts, decisions still outstanding and Seren's own wishes. Ask what will happen on the first day without school transport. Record actual arrangements and unresolved responsibility rather than an optimistic statement that “transition is underway”.

## Recording check

Capture the child's account, family context, caring responsibilities, source of concerns, relevant frameworks, education-plan links, information shared, named actions and dates. Record disagreements, missing information and escalation arrangements. Keep cross-border placements and responsibility questions explicit in the handover.
