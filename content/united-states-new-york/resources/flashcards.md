## Professional Foundations — New York


### Which local framework anchors this topic in New York practice guide?

New York Education Law and the Office of the Professions distinguish Licensed Master Social Workers (LMSWs) from Licensed Clinical Social Workers (LCSWs). An LMSW may provide clinical social-work services, including diagnosis, psychotherapy and assessment-based treatment planning, only under the required supervision; an LCSW may provide those services independently within competence.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

Licensure, registration, a permitted practice setting and any separate mental-hygiene designation must be checked. Employer seniority does not create a statutory power. Professional ethical guidance should be read alongside state law and applicable agency obligations.


### What are the key local actions?

Confirm the licence, current registration, scope and supervision required for the actual work. Explain the professional role, service purpose and confidentiality limits. Check any additional designation or qualification required for a statutory mental-health function. Use supervision and continuing education to improve the effect of practice, including cultural responsiveness and access.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

The Office of the Professions addresses professional regulation; benefit decisions, agency services and mental-health detention have different complaint or review routes. Explain which body can address the person’s concern and desired remedy.


### What common error should you avoid?

Treating LMSW and LCSW practice permissions as interchangeable.


## Social Care and Support — New York


### Which local framework anchors this topic in New York practice guide?

Medicaid personal care and Consumer Directed Personal Assistance Services involve assessment, medical necessity and service authorisation. The New York Independent Assessor Program (NYIAP), local departments of social services and managed-care organisations have different responsibilities. An assessment does not itself authorise every requested hour of care.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

Current Department of Health guidance includes minimum-needs requirements introduced in 2025 and legacy-status arrangements. Check the person’s programme and circumstances before applying a new rule or assuming that an existing recipient follows the same route as a new applicant.


### What are the key local actions?

Identify Medicaid eligibility, the service requested and the responsible assessment and authorisation bodies. Obtain the applicable community assessment and practitioner information through the current NYIAP process. Confirm the plan of care, amount, duration, frequency and actual provider arrangements. Record voluntary informal support, unmet needs and the notice, plan appeal or fair-hearing route for a disputed decision.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Managed-care appeals and state fair hearings depend on the decision and programme. Read the notice for time limits, any required internal step and continued-benefit rules. A complaint to the provider is different from challenging the authorisation.


### What common error should you avoid?

Confusing NYIAP assessment with final service authorisation or overlooking current minimum-needs and legacy rules.


## Decision-Making and Capacity — New York


### Which local framework anchors this topic in New York practice guide?

Healthcare proxies, the Family Health Care Decisions Act (FHCDA) and Mental Hygiene Law Article 81 guardianship are distinct. Check the healthcare setting and statutory scope before using the FHCDA surrogate list. A healthcare proxy or a valid patient decision may need consideration before a default surrogate route. FHCDA capacity concerns understanding and appreciating what the proposed care involves and its consequences, including risks, benefits and alternatives, and making an informed choice. Use the statutory assessment process; a social-work opinion alone does not replace a required practitioner determination.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

Article 81 requires a necessity assessment and, where incapacity is alleged, clear and convincing evidence about inability to meet personal or property needs and insufficient understanding and appreciation of that inability, with likely harm. The court should tailor powers and consider available alternatives. A diagnosis alone does not establish the guardianship test.


### What are the key local actions?

Specify the decision, support the person and identify the applicable healthcare or guardianship framework. Check any proxy, prior decision or court order and the scope of authority. Where FHCDA applies, use the statutory surrogate priority, eligibility and decision standards. For an Article 81 issue, document functional evidence, likely harm and less restrictive available resources.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

The FHCDA includes requirements for surrogate decisions, with additional safeguards for life-sustaining treatment. Article 81 has court procedures and tailored powers. Disagreement about a healthcare choice should not automatically become a request for broad guardianship.


### What common error should you avoid?

Treating family status as priority over every valid appointment or using diagnosis alone to establish Article 81 incapacity.


## Restrictions and Liberty — New York


### Which local framework anchors this topic in New York practice guide?

Mental Hygiene Law Article 9 admission, Article 81 guardianship and facility restraint rules concern different authorities. An Article 81 guardian receives only the powers granted by the court. The personal-needs provisions do not make guardianship a general permission for involuntary psychiatric admission.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

Separate a placement decision, the actual restriction on departure and the authority for treatment. An institution should identify the legal basis for each measure and the required review. A family’s agreement or an administrative discharge concern does not establish detention authority.


### What are the key local actions?

Describe the restriction, setting, person’s wishes and current legal status. Read the actual Article 9 documents or Article 81 order and identify its limits. Check separate authority and safeguards for restraint or treatment. Arrange Mental Hygiene Legal Service or other appropriate legal support and the relevant court or complaint process.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

A court challenge to detention, review of guardianship powers and a service complaint address different matters. Explain the route relevant to the actual restriction. The person should have accessible rights information and help to express and act on objections.


### What common error should you avoid?

Treating a guardian’s placement power as a universal psychiatric detention power.


## Mental Health — New York


### Which local framework anchors this topic in New York practice guide?

Mental Hygiene Law Article 9 provides distinct voluntary, emergency and other involuntary-admission routes. Section 9.39 concerns immediate hospital observation, care and treatment where the mental-illness and likely-serious-harm conditions are met. Its current definition includes specified inability or refusal, because of mental illness, to meet essential needs causing substantial risk of physical harm.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

Under this route, retention beyond 48 hours requires confirmation by another physician from the psychiatric staff; the emergency period is limited to 15 days. Further retention requires the applicable additional process. These are route-specific limits, not rules to apply indiscriminately to every Article 9 admission.


### What are the key local actions?

Identify the exact Article 9 route and evidence for its current criteria. Check the authorised practitioner, examinations, forms, notices and timing. Use the current OMH guidance, including the updated essential-needs wording, and consider voluntary alternatives. Separate detention from treatment authority and coordinate accepted discharge and community-support arrangements.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Section 9.39 requires rights notice and provides a court-hearing route, with Mental Hygiene Legal Service involvement. Different statuses have different review procedures. A treatment-over-objection issue needs its own analysis and should not be assumed resolved by admission.


### What common error should you avoid?

Using an outdated threat-only account of serious harm or confusing the time limits of different admission routes.


## Adult Safeguarding — New York


### Which local framework anchors this topic in New York practice guide?

Social Services Law section 473 provides protective services for adults meeting its conditions, without regard to income. The framework concerns mental or physical impairments affecting ability to manage resources, daily living or protection from harm, together with the absence of someone available, willing and able to assist responsibly.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

Local social-services districts provide adult-protective services and coordinate with health, mental-health, legal and other agencies. A general APS referral, a setting-specific mandated report and an application for guardianship are different matters. Do not invent a universal adult-reporting duty from the child-reporting rules.


### What are the key local actions?

Identify the harm, functional difficulties and whether responsible assistance is actually available. Refer through the appropriate local adult-protective services route and address immediate danger. Check any separate provider, facility, police or mandated-reporting requirement. Use the least restrictive appropriate response and distinguish voluntary services from court-authorised measures.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

The adult’s agreement and participation remain important; an APS referral does not confer general authority to force services. Where a court measure is proposed, identify its own conditions and representation. Explain the local service complaint and relevant legal route.


### What common error should you avoid?

Assuming that any listed relative rules out APS support or that a referral automatically authorises guardianship.


## Children, Families and Transitions — New York


### Which local framework anchors this topic in New York practice guide?

Social Services Law sections 413 and 415 identify mandated reporters and reporting procedures. Covered social workers must act on reasonable cause to suspect abuse or maltreatment within the statutory circumstances. The Statewide Central Register route has defined scope, including the relationship of the alleged responsible person to the child.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

An immediate report and the required written follow-up are distinct steps; section 415 generally requires the written report within 48 hours. Abuse by someone outside the child-protective reporting definition may still require police or another safety response. Do not treat an out-of-scope SCR decision as proof that no action is needed.


### What are the key local actions?

Identify the reporter role, child’s age, alleged responsible person and facts supporting reasonable cause. Use the required SCR reporting process and immediate emergency response where necessary. Complete the applicable written follow-up and document receipt, facts and safety actions. Consider police, institutional or other reporting duties separately and coordinate family support.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

Explain the child-protective process and access to support or legal advice. Reporting is not a court determination of abuse or authority for every intervention. Preserve the child’s account and the distinction between an allegation, investigation and finding.


### What common error should you avoid?

Confusing reasonable cause with proof, or assuming the SCR is the only possible route for every threat to a child.


## Rights, Culture and Recording — New York


### Which local framework anchors this topic in New York practice guide?

Federal civil-rights and disability law, New York Human Rights Law and sector-specific patient rights apply according to the service and decision. Identify the responsible body and the actual access barrier or restriction rather than assuming every complaint uses the same route.


### Which distinction must be kept clear?

HIPAA applies to covered entities and business associates; Mental Hygiene Law section 33.13 protects covered clinical records, and Part 2 provides additional requirements for certain substance-use-disorder records. The 2024 Part 2 changes required compliance by February 2026. Determine which overlapping rules apply before sharing.


### What are the key local actions?

Identify the organisation, record and state or federal privacy provisions involved. Arrange accessible communication, interpretation and culturally responsive participation. Check the legal basis, purpose and scope of disclosure, including any special rules for proceedings. Explain records access, correction, complaints and the applicable patient-rights or legal review route.


### How can the person participate or challenge the decision?

A privacy complaint, professional complaint and mental-hygiene rights challenge address different questions. A request from police, a lawyer or another service does not itself establish authority to release a whole record. Obtain the relevant advice and document the decision.


### What common error should you avoid?

Checking HIPAA while overlooking state mental-hygiene or specially protected substance-use records.
