# Social Care and Support — Aotearoa New Zealand

## Learning outcomes

Map assessment, eligibility, funding and delivery separately; include family support without assuming it; explain the local route for challenging a decision.

## Local legislation, policy and professional framework

Disability Support Services (DSS), Needs Assessment and Service Coordination (NASC), Enabling Good Lives arrangements, health services and ACC have different eligibility and funding roles. Identify the scheme relevant to the person’s circumstances; injury-related support, disability support and older-person services should not be treated as one entitlement.

DSS guidance describes referral to a NASC provider to understand needs and determine eligibility, with relevant supporting evidence. A needs assessment, allocation of funding and a service beginning are separate events. Check the current assessment and funding arrangements rather than relying on an old agency name or contract.

## Assess daily life, not just a list of services

Begin with what a good day would look like. Explore washing, dressing, nutrition, medicines, mobility, home safety, relationships, work or education and community participation. Ask about variation: what happens at night, on a bad day or when a regular supporter is unavailable? Establish what the person can do with appropriate equipment, accessible information or a different environment. Do not confuse performing a task once during a visit with being able to do it reliably in daily life.

Speak with family supporters about willingness, practical ability and sustainability. Their account belongs alongside the person's account, not in place of it. Record support that is actually offered and its limits. An adult child working overnight is not an available overnight care service. Where views differ, describe the disagreement and its consequences for the proposed plan.

## Turn the assessment into a deliverable plan

Keep four decisions visible: the needs identified; the applicable eligibility decision; the funding or charging decision; and the service that will actually start. A referral, waiting-list entry or funding approval is not proof that support has begun. Obtain the person's understanding of costs and responsibilities before recording agreement.

For each outcome, identify an action, a responsible service or person and a review trigger. Include what happens if the worker does not arrive, the person declines support, a carer becomes unavailable or needs change. Record unmet needs and interim arrangements explicitly. A budget dispute should not disappear into a description of the person as difficult or unrealistic.

## Apply the local pathway

- Identify the responsible funder and referral route, including NASC or another applicable service.
- Describe the person’s goals, daily support needs, environmental barriers and existing support.
- Check the evidence needed for eligibility and explain the decision and funding allocation separately.
- Confirm the provider, start date, whānau willingness and contingency for gaps or changing needs.

## Rights, challenge and review

Ask the relevant assessment or funding body for reasons and the applicable reassessment, review or complaint route. DSS publishes current assessment information. Track disputes with different funders separately and do not describe a referral to another agency as a resolved need.

## Worked learning example

Fictional case: A person with a longstanding disability develops additional support needs after an injury, and two services each suggest the other should pay.

Before reading the discussion, identify the relevant decision, the missing evidence and the person who can lawfully act.

### Discussion and reasoning

Map the needs and the scope of each scheme, obtain the actual eligibility decisions and coordinate the interface. Record interim support and unresolved funding responsibility. Keep the person’s desired outcomes visible while the agencies resolve the dispute.

### Common error to avoid

Assuming that one diagnosis, one assessment or one funding approval covers every service need.

## Recording and supervision exercise

Use the matching printable prompt to make a short record for this case. Include:

- Daily-life evidence, fluctuation and desired outcomes
- The service, eligibility test and decision-maker
- Carer willingness and separately identified needs
- Funding, charges, decisions and reasons
- Start dates, unmet needs, contingency and challenge route

Ask a supervisor to identify a missing fact that could change your conclusion. Revise the plan to show who will obtain that fact and what happens while it remains uncertain.
