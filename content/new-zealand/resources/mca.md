# Decision-Making and Capacity — Aotearoa New Zealand

## Learning outcomes

Identify the precise decision, provide practical decision support, apply the local capacity or consent framework and verify a representative’s authority.

## Local legislation, policy and professional framework

Right 7 of the Code of Health and Disability Services Consumers’ Rights addresses informed choice and consent, presumes competence and preserves choice to the extent of a person’s competence. The Protection of Personal and Property Rights Act 1988 (PPPR) provides routes including welfare guardianship, property orders and enduring powers of attorney.

Where a consumer is not competent and no legally entitled representative is available, Right 7(4) has a specific framework. It is not a general rule that any relative may sign. Read the provision and the particular appointment before identifying who may act and what decision standard applies.

## Start with the decision and the support

Write the decision as a question the person could answer: accepting a particular treatment, choosing accommodation or authorising a financial transaction. These questions may engage different laws and decision-makers. A diagnosis, cognitive score or disagreement with professionals is not a complete capacity assessment. Describe the ability relevant to this decision and the evidence for any concern.

Prepare information about the real options, including doing nothing where relevant. Use an interpreter, accessible material, communication aids and suitable timing. Address pain, fatigue, sensory impairment, medication effects and anxiety. Ask the person to explain their understanding in their own way; repeating professional phrases is not the only way to demonstrate understanding. Record what support was tried and its effect, rather than simply writing that all support was offered.

## Keep authority and the decision itself separate

A relative may provide valuable information without holding authority to decide. Obtain the actual appointment, agreement, court order or statutory basis being relied upon. Check the decisions it covers, conditions, whether it is operative and whether another person has priority. Authority over property does not automatically include healthcare or accommodation.

Where a representative can lawfully act, identify the decision standard they must apply locally. Explore the person's wishes, values, previous statements and present participation. Record a disagreement rather than converting it into apparent agreement. Consider whether a reversible interim option allows more support or recovery before an irreversible decision. If authority remains disputed, obtain the appropriate clinical or legal advice and arrange the applicable review; a multidisciplinary meeting cannot create missing legal powers.

## Apply the local pathway

- Specify the treatment or other decision and provide the support needed for participation.
- Identify the applicable consent or PPPR framework and record decision-specific evidence.
- Verify the scope and operation of any enduring power of attorney or court order.
- If relying on Right 7(4), check its conditions, efforts to ascertain the person’s views and consultation requirements.

## Rights, challenge and review

HDC and the independent advocacy service provide routes concerning consumer rights. PPPR court orders and representative disputes may require Family Court or other legal advice. The appropriate review depends on whether the issue is a service breach, capacity decision or representative’s authority.

## Worked learning example

Fictional case: A woman can express a clear preference about treatment but needs information presented in short sessions. Her son says he should sign because he manages her bills.

Before reading the discussion, identify the relevant decision, the missing evidence and the person who can lawfully act.

### Discussion and reasoning

Provide the communication support and assess the relevant decision. Financial help does not establish healthcare authority. Read any appointment and use the applicable consent framework, preserving the woman’s own choices to the extent she can make them.

### Common error to avoid

Treating diminished competence as loss of every choice or assuming financial authority includes healthcare.

## Recording and supervision exercise

Use the matching printable prompt to make a short record for this case. Include:

- Decision, options and time at which it is needed
- Communication support and observed responses
- Applicable legal test and evidence for each element
- Representative’s document, scope and decision standard
- Wishes, disagreement, review and any urgent action

Ask a supervisor to identify a missing fact that could change your conclusion. Revise the plan to show who will obtain that fact and what happens while it remains uncertain.
