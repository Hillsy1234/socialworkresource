# Practice Glossary — Victoria practice guide

Use each definition with its linked full lesson and official sources.

## Professional Foundations

The Australian Association of Social Workers (AASW) Code of Ethics and Practice Standards provide a professional framework. AASW membership or accreditation, an employer requirement and statutory authority are different matters. Check the requirements for the actual role and state rather than presenting the professional association as the source of every legal power.

## Social Care and Support

Disability support through the NDIS, state health and housing services, and Commonwealth aged care have separate entry and funding rules. NDIS access involves age, residence and disability or early-intervention requirements; a diagnosis alone does not establish entitlement to a particular funded support. Check current NDIA rules for the actual application or plan.

## Decision-Making and Capacity

The Medical Treatment Planning and Decisions Act 2016 governs important medical-consent and advance-care-directive questions. The Guardianship and Administration Act 2019 provides a separate framework for guardianship, administration and supported appointments. Specify whether the decision concerns treatment, personal matters or finances. Medical decision-making capacity concerns understanding the relevant information and effects, retaining it for the decision, considering it when choosing, and conveying the choice and related views or needs by any workable means. Provide practical support first; an apparently unwise choice does not establish incapacity.

## Restrictions and Liberty

The Disability Act 2006 and Victorian Senior Practitioner arrangements operate alongside the NDIS restrictive-practices framework where applicable. Authorised Program Officers, the Senior Practitioner, behaviour support practitioners and implementing providers have different responsibilities. A plan is not proof that every necessary authorisation or approval exists.

## Mental Health

The Mental Health and Wellbeing Act 2022 replaced the 2014 Act. It prefers voluntary assessment and treatment and provides separate assessment orders, temporary treatment orders and Mental Health Tribunal treatment orders. Each stage has its own authorised decision-maker and requirements.

## Adult Safeguarding

Adult concerns may require family-violence services, police, OPA, disability safeguards, aged-care oversight or a provider complaint. Identify the relationship and service setting before selecting a route. The Family Violence Protection Act 2008 is relevant to violence and abuse within its defined relationships; not every adult concern is governed by that framework.

## Children, Families and Transitions

The Children, Youth and Families Act 2005 distinguishes protection grounds and mandatory reporting. The mandatory-reporter categories depend on the role: do not assume every person called a social worker is included solely by title. A social worker may fall within a listed role, such as school counselling or out-of-home care work.

## Rights, Culture and Recording

The Charter of Human Rights and Responsibilities Act 2006 requires covered public authorities to act compatibly with Charter rights and give proper consideration to relevant rights, subject to statutory exceptions. Identify whether the provider is a public authority for the function involved. A Charter statement should explain the affected rights and reasoning, not simply declare compliance.

## Australian Association of Social Workers (AASW)

The professional body identified for this guide. Check its remit and the requirements that apply to your role.

## Advocacy

Support to express views, understand choices and participate; statutory entitlements depend on the local framework.

## CPD

Continuing professional development: learning, reflection and evidence of improvement.

## Consent

Agreement to a specific action; check the applicable requirements for information, voluntariness and ability to consent.

## Coercion

Pressure or control that can affect expressed wishes and freedom to act.

## Eligibility

The criteria for a particular service or programme; distinct from a description of need.

## Capacity

Ability to make the relevant decision under the applicable legal framework; avoid a global label.

## Representative

Someone with a defined support or decision-making role; verify the source and scope of authority.

## Proportionality

A reasoned comparison of intended benefit, burdens, rights and alternatives.

## Cultural safety

Attention to power, identity and the person’s experience of a service, informed by their preferences.

## Data minimisation

Using or sharing only information needed for an identified lawful purpose.

## Confidentiality

Protecting information while understanding and explaining lawful exceptions.

## Safeguarding referral

Communicating a concern to the appropriate agency; confirm the applicable route and follow-up.

## Professional supervision

Structured discussion of decisions, ethics, skills, wellbeing and accountability.

## Formulation

A reasoned account connecting evidence and possible explanations to a plan.

## Family network

Relationships that may offer support; availability, safety and willingness need assessment.

## Transition

Planned change between services or life stages, with continuity and named responsibilities.

## Contingency

An agreed response if risks increase or the preferred plan cannot operate.

## Review

A planned check of outcomes, changing circumstances and whether actions remain appropriate.

## Least restrictive option

An option that can achieve the purpose with less interference in rights and freedom.
